## Abstract

**Background.** The heliosphere is a dynamically generated interface between a stellar outflow and its external plasma environment. At galactic scale, several phenomena are already independently established: galactic winds transport mass, energy and metals beyond galaxies; the circumgalactic medium (CGM) is multiphase and exchanges mass, momentum and energy with galaxies; and magnetic fields and cosmic rays influence these interactions.

**Hypothesis.** We investigate whether these apparently separate galactic phenomena can be treated as components of a higher-order distributed galactic interaction boundary—a functional analogue of the heliosphere—which we provisionally call the **galactosphere**. The hypothesis does not require a sharp physical shell. Instead, it predicts a scale-dependent transition zone in which outward galactic transport progressively loses dominance to the surrounding circumgalactic/intergalactic environment.

**Digital laboratory.** We constructed a dimensionless radial multi-field experiment representing competing outward transport and environmental confinement, with coupled thermal, magnetic/nonthermal and ambient-medium terms. Parameter sweeps tested whether the transition remains localized to a single radius or becomes spatially distributed when multiple physical channels are coupled. The experiment was deliberately formulated as a structural model rather than a fitted Milky Way simulation.

**Plain findings (digital sandbox · seed `20261007` · suite E8):**

1. **GH-1 independent channels:** Transition width (span where dominance ratio $\mathcal{D}\in[0.5,2]$) ≈ **0.110**; channel half-drop radii coincide (spread ≈ 0).
2. **GH-1 coupled channels:** Width ≈ **0.350** (~3.2× independent); characteristic $\mathcal{D}=1$ crossing ≈ **1.37** $R_0$; channel radii separate — kinematic ≈ **1.00**, thermal ≈ **1.17**, magnetic ≈ **1.40**, cosmic-ray/nonthermal ≈ **1.63**.
3. **GH-1 sweep (n=48):** Mean width ≈ **0.364**, crossing std ≈ **0.142**; interface never eliminated (min width ≈ **0.280**). Changing channel weights moves location and width rather than deleting the transition.
4. **GH-2 cross-scale topology:** Planet / star / galaxy / cluster-labeled normalized systems share finite-width topology (mean width ≈ **0.370**, max relative deviation ≈ **0.108**). Cluster-scale interface is left **unnamed**.

**Novelty relative to known work.** Individual ingredients are not novel. The potentially novel contribution is their cross-scale organization as a single boundary-function hypothesis, motivated by the heliosphere/galactosphere analogy and tested through a dimensionless digital experiment.

**Implications.** If supported by observational and high-fidelity numerical tests, the framework predicts searches should identify coordinated radial changes across multiple observables rather than a single galactopause radius. Digital status: **PROMISING — NOT YET ASTROPHYSICALLY VALIDATED**.

> Can a galaxy’s exchange with its surroundings be understood as a distributed interaction boundary — a galactosphere — without requiring a sharp shell?

### 0.1 Setup — dominance ratio

Dimensionless radius $r/R_0$. Competing contributions:

$$
W(r)=\text{outward galactic transport},\quad
T(r)=\text{thermal},\quad
B(r)=\text{magnetic},\quad
C(r)=\text{cosmic-ray/nonthermal},\quad
E(r)=\text{external environment}
$$

$$
\mathcal{D}(r)=\frac{W+T+B+C}{E}
$$

Conventional single-radius “boundary” ≈ $\mathcal{D}(r)=1$. The expedition asks whether the system changes state at one radius or across a finite region. Transition **width** = radial span where $\mathcal{D}\in[0.5,2]$.

## 1. The central proposition

The key move is not:

> “The CGM is the galactic heliosphere.”

That is too simplistic.

The proposition is:

> A galaxy may possess a **distributed galactic interaction boundary** whose function is analogous to the heliosphere: a dynamically maintained transition between internally generated outward transport and the surrounding medium.

We call the functional system:

**Galactosphere**

Not necessarily a sphere.  
Not necessarily a sharp shell.  
Not necessarily a single physical surface.

Rather:

Galaxy → galactic wind/outflow → multiphase transition → CGM → external/intergalactic environment

The CGM literature already describes multiphase fuel supply, feedback, recycling and regulation. Hot and cool phases exchange mass, energy and momentum; magnetic fields and cosmic rays can modify those interactions. We are not inventing those observations. We are asking whether they constitute a higher-order **boundary function**.

## 5. The strongest prediction (P1)

If a galactosphere exists as a functional distributed interface, several observables should exhibit **coordinated** radial transitions, rather than independent unrelated gradients:

- outflow velocity  
- gas density  
- temperature  
- ionization state  
- metallicity  
- magnetic-field structure  
- cosmic-ray pressure/transport  
- inflow/outflow balance  

Transition radii need not coincide. The hypothesis predicts they probably should not:

$$
r_{\mathrm{thermal}} \neq r_{\mathrm{magnetic}} \neq r_{\mathrm{CR}} \neq r_{\mathrm{kinematic}}
$$

while occupying a correlated radial domain. That is the galactic equivalent of recognizing that a heliosphere is not adequately described by one scalar boundary alone.

## 7. Falsification criteria (F1–F6)

The galactosphere hypothesis would be weakened if:

1. **F1** — no coherent multi-observable transition exists;  
2. **F2** — the apparent transition is entirely explained by the virial radius with no additional structure;  
3. **F3** — magnetic/nonthermal components do not materially participate;  
4. **F4** — different galaxies show no common dimensionless topology;  
5. **F5** — high-resolution simulations show the apparent boundary is merely an artifact of parameterization;  
6. **F6** — the heliosphere analogy adds no predictive power beyond existing CGM terminology.

It becomes stronger if independent observations and simulations repeatedly reveal coherent, scale-dependent, multiphase transition zones coupled to galactic outflow and environmental interaction.
