## Abstract

Different employers, benchmarks, and vocabularies still produce research that **rhymes** on three structural families: **multiscale / self-similar organization** (Fractal lane), **part–whole / local–global encoding with deliberate expansion and reduction** (Holographic lane), and **bounded adaptation inside a viable compute or reliability band** (Goldilocks lane). This Homeostasis Expedition application companion asks whether those rhymes are coincidence, shared priors, or something testable when systems are scored **after** discovery without FHG words in their objectives.

**Method (two layers, not to be conflated).** Layer A is a literature synthesis centered on six independent anchors: fractal complexity of language and LLM outputs (Alabdulmohsin & Steiner, ICML 2025); component-level fractal consistency for reasoning (Luo et al., ICML 2026); holographic node embeddings (Bevilacqua et al., ICLR 2025); hypothesis-path expansion and reduction under budget (Qiu et al., HyPER, ICML 2026); optimal self-consistency scaling and dynamic sample allocation (Feng et al., ICML 2026); and recursive self-improvement of research agents under hidden evaluation (Srikanth et al., arXiv:2609.26457). Layer B is the **Convergence Layer Test**: an integrated FHG panel (P1–P6) executed in the **Hero Leo Computational Lab** as deterministic fixtures reusing blind agentic optimizers — post-hoc \(S_F, S_H, S_G\) subscores only; **not** a re-host of external benchmark code.

**Findings (qualified).** The synthesis finds recurring but uneven structural rhymes: multiscale statistics beyond perplexity, component-wise agreement, expand–reduce control, Pareto bands on test-time compute, and harness-level self-improvement with held-out gates. **Publication timing:** all six anchors entered the public record within roughly eighteen months (ICLR / ICML 2025 through late-2026 preprint), with a **three-paper ICML 2026 cluster** on adaptive test-time reasoning — a descriptive acceleration signal, not a bibliometric proof (Section 8.1). Those rhymes do **not** yet validate a single unified FHG battery or prove a universal attractor.

**Keywords:** independent research convergence; fractal; holographic; Goldilocks; literature synthesis; test-time compute; self-consistency; Homeostasis Expedition; falsifiability.

## 1. Introduction

Machine learning research has never been a single road. One group measures bits-per-token fractal structure in human and model text; another decomposes reasoning outputs into components that can agree at different granularities; a third pre-trains graph nodes through expansion and symmetry-aware reduction; a fourth reallocates test-time compute between exploring hypothesis paths and exploiting promising branches; a fifth derives power-law scaling for self-consistency and allocates samples adaptively; a sixth closes a loop in which a research agent rewrites its own harness and keeps only changes that pass hidden benchmarks.

The methods differ. The loss functions differ. The adjectives in the abstracts rarely align.

Yet when the FractiAI research program reviewed these independent threads through October 14, 2026, a pattern kept returning — not as shared branding, but as **structural claims** about what useful systems must preserve under noise and cost:

1. **Fractal (multiscale) lane:** information and dynamics look self-similar across granularities; local statistics mirror longer scope when measurement is done carefully.
2. **Holographic (part–whole) lane:** local representations retain or reconstruct global structure through deliberate expansion and reduction maps.
3. **Goldilocks (viable band) lane:** systems stay useful by adapting inside bounds — neither freezing after the first shock nor spending unbounded compute on redundant exploration.

This paper names that triad **FHG** for scoring and expedition coordination. It does **not** claim FHG is the hidden law of nature or that every serious paper endorses Infinite Octaves catalog grammar.

The scientific question is narrower and harder:

**Independent Research Convergence (IRC-FHG):** When research groups or systems optimize useful information under noise and cost **without** being instructed to find fractals, holograms, or Goldilocks bands, do their invented structures and behaviors converge post-hoc on FHG property scores more than matched flat or wasteful controls — while remaining **non-identical** (convergence without photocopying)?

## 2. Problem statement

### 2.1 Two failure modes of “convergence” talk

**Failure mode A — branding convergence.** If evaluators inject framework vocabulary into prompts (“find fractal structure”), agreement proves instruction-following, not discovery.

**Failure mode B — metric tourism.** If a synthesis author merges incompatible “Goldilocks” objects (Hurst bands, sample budgets, harness grades) into one headline chart, the map looks unified while nothing commensurate was measured.

IRC-FHG refuses both. It treats convergence as an empirical claim about **blind** search and **post-hoc** scoring.

### 2.2 Why this belongs in Homeostasis Expedition

Homeostasis Expedition work studies regulation, viable bands, and honest nulls on the map. IRC-FHG connects that narrative spine to **independent AI research** — hiring parsers that drift, budgeting assistants that hide lease lines, and research agents that rewrite policy until compliance officers lose the thread. FHG is a compact checklist for those failures: multiscale drift, part–whole betrayal, and adaptation outside a trustworthy band.

## 4. Central hypotheses

**H1 — Literary (modest).** Independent research threads that optimize prediction, compression, verifiable reasoning, or research efficiency under cost should **rhyme** with FHG structural claims more often than chance-like pairings — even when abstracts share no vocabulary.

**H2 — Empirical (sharp; integrated test).** On matched tasks and budgets, systems scored blind to FHG vocabulary should exceed flat or wasteful controls on mean post-hoc FHG property scores by pre-registered margins, while pairwise outputs stay below an identity ceiling.

**H3 — Protocol necessity.** If adaptive, expand–reduce, component-wise, and hidden-eval disciplines add no measurable FHG margin over naive baselines once vocabulary is locked out, the IRC-FHG protocol adds nothing beyond noise.

**H4 — Anti-universal attractor.** If convergence appears only when evaluators re-introduce FHG branding into objectives, the “all roads” hypothesis fails before any large-scale replication spend.

### 6.2 Fractal lane — component-level consistency for reasoning

| **Adaptive Stopping Criteria Calibration (ASCC)** adjusts sampling to task difficulty and model capability | **G** | Dynamic compute band instead of static sample counts |
| Up to ~55% sample-cost reduction with competitive accuracy on QA benchmarks | **G** | Pareto improvement under fixed quality floor — viable band, not “more samples always” |

| Claim (summarized) | FHG lane | IRC-FHG use |

## 7. Cross-thread narrative (what rhymes, without forcing unity)

Reading the six anchors in sequence, three rhymes recur — each with different measurement objects.

**Fractal rhyme.** Alabdulmohsin & Steiner show that fractal parameters in **text** carry information perplexity misses. Luo et al. apply “fractal” to **component-wise** agreement in reasoning outputs — a different scale axis, but the same refusal to treat monolithic scores as sufficient. Feng et al. add **scaling laws** for how reliability grows with samples — fractal in the statistical sense of lawful scaling across budget, not spatial self-similarity.

**Holographic rhyme.** Bevilacqua et al. make expansion and reduction **explicit** in embedding space. Qiu et al. make expand–reduce **explicit** in hypothesis-path space. Luo et al. implicitly holograph reasoning by letting **parts** of answers vote before the **whole** is fixed. Srikanth et al. treat the **harness** as a whole edited through local code changes that must preserve global benchmark behavior.

**Goldilocks rhyme.** Narrow natural-language fractal bands vs wide LLM bands (Alabdulmohsin & Steiner); adaptive stopping and task-aware sampling (Luo; Feng); Pareto compute fronts (Qiu); hidden-eval acceptance (Srikanth). Each paper names a different “band object.” Section 9 lists why those objects must not be merged naively.

None of the six papers claim FHG branding. IRC-FHG asks whether a **blind scorer** would tag them similarly anyway.

## 9. Disagreements, false friends, and metric incommensurability

### 9.1 Four “Goldilocks” objects that must not be merged

| Community object | What “band” means | Merge risk |
|------------------|-------------------|------------|
| Natural-language fractal parameter band | Hurst / scaling stats in human text | Collapsing into QA sample budgets |
| Adaptive self-consistency / fractal-consistency stopping | Dynamic sample count per item | Collapsing into graph embedding reuse |
| HyPER token Pareto front | Exploration–exploitation under fixed TTS budget | Collapsing into harness hidden-eval grades |
| AIDE² hidden selection grade | Accepted rewrite threshold on private tasks | Collapsing into perplexity or F1 alone |

IRC-FHG scorers must declare **which band family** a metric belongs to before comparing cohorts.

### 9.2 Three “holographic” objects

| Usage | Meaning in thread | Not equivalent to |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Bevilacqua et al. | Expansion/reduction embedding blueprint | AdS/CFT |
| Qiu et al. | Path pool expand–reduce on hypotheses | Optical holograms |
| Catalog / Soft Story | Cross-scale reconstructability rhyme | Prompted “holographic thinking” |

### 9.3 Three “fractal” objects

| Usage | Meaning | Pitfall |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Alabdulmohsin & Steiner | Statistical self-similarity in information rate | Calling all deep nets “fractal” without measurement |
| Luo et al. | Component-tree consistency | Confusing with Hurst exponents in text |
| Feng et al. | Power-law scaling of SC error | Confusing with spatial fractal dimension of weights |

### 9.4 Independence vs identity

Convergence requires elevated **overlap of property scores**, not identical outputs. Identity collapse (near-duplicate trajectories or harnesses) falsifies interesting convergence — it indicates shared prompts, copy-paste, or trivial replication.

## 10. Integrated FHG convergence test (Layer B — computational lab executed)

### 10.1 Design intent

The integrated test **does not** re-brand the six papers as participants in a FractiAI suite. It **does** ask: on a **pre-registered panel** of tasks drawn from the same **problem classes** those papers address (text generation quality, QA reasoning, graph transfer, test-time scaling, sample allocation, harness improvement), do systems optimized under **blind** objectives (no FHG tokens) produce outputs whose **post-hoc** FHG subscores co-move more than matched controls?

Controls are **wasteful baselines**, not sycophancy prompts: fixed-sample self-consistency where adaptivity is possible; monolithic answer voting where component consistency is possible; fixed tree width where expand–reduce is possible; open-loop harness edits where hidden-eval gates exist.

### 10.2 Panel sketch (representative, not exhaustive)

| Panel slot | Inspired by anchor | Blind objective (no FHG words) | Primary post-hoc FHG probes |
|------------|-------------------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| P1 — Text stats | Alabdulmohsin & Steiner | Minimize perplexity / maximize fluency under decoding sweep | \(S_F\): fractal-parameter distance to human band; \(S_G\): band violation rate |
| P2 — Component QA | Luo et al. | Maximize accuracy under **fixed** sample cap | \(S_F\): component agreement curves; \(S_G\): cost at fixed accuracy vs static SC |
