Teaching a model to read the genome’s most stubborn repeats

SS Vibelandia — 2026-06-17 · Fair Exchange

For decades, the centers of human chromosomes were a blur: dense alpha-satellite arrays that defeated both classical sequence models and the newest neural stacks. The Telomere-to-Telomere assembly finally opened those regions. The harder question was how to model them without either exploding a Hidden Markov Model’s state space or drowning a Transformer in low-entropy noise.

The Active Context Hidden Markov Model — AC-HMM — keeps classical transitions intact and injects deterministic, history-derived context into the emission side. Exact forward-backward inference stays. Baum-Welch stays. The parameter bill drops, because context is looked up rather than reinvented as another stochastic layer.

Centromeric High-Order Repeats shape chromosomal identity and segregation; structural variation there is medically relevant; and until T2T-CHM13, much of the sequence itself was missing. A model that can score those landscapes out of sample — and transfer frozen parameters across chromosomes — is a usable instrument for assemblies that finally include the hard parts.

The paper evaluates spatial folds on public UCSC DNA from the T2T reference, reports log-likelihood gains under cross-chromosomal transfer (Chr11 → ChrX), and runs Leave-One-HOR-Out holdouts. Equally important is the pre-registered failure zone: high-entropy retrotransposon insertions that break the periodic inductive bias. Naming where the method loses is how reproducible genomics stays welcome in serious rooms.
