Research note · Agentic Convergence

If Nobody Mentions Fractals, Do Independent Agents Still Find the Cheap Path?

FractiAI Research — October 2, 2026

When a paycheck depends on a forecast that keeps eating compute, the real fight is rarely the slogan on the slide deck. It is whether the next model finds a shorter route through noisy numbers without inventing a private religion. The Agentic Convergence Experiment asks a sharper version of that fight: if you hand several independent optimizers the same raw observations, forbid them from being told what poetry to find, and score only useful prediction per unit of work, do their invented representations still rhyme — after the fact — with multiscale compression, hierarchical coarse-to-fine encoding, local measurements that keep global information, and bounded recursive adaptation?

The problem with testing a favorite word

It is easy to “prove” a framework by whispering the answer into the prompt. Tell an agent to hunt for fractals and it will dutifully produce something fractal-shaped. That result mostly measures obedience. Parents juggling school forms, rent due dates, and a phone full of “smart” summaries already know the failure mode: systems that sound certain because they were steered, not because they discovered a cheaper path through the mess.

The experiment flips the instruction. Agents share one objective in plain language — maximize useful predictive information extracted per unit of observation and computation while keeping predictive performance — and they never receive the words fractal, holographic, golden-ratio nesting, Infinite Octave, Goldilocks, or recursive homeostasis. Different architectures and seeds explore in isolation. They do not see one another’s notes. What we keep is the path, not only the final chart.

How the run is built

Phase one builds a shared landscape: nested pulses under noise, local patches that still carry a global envelope, amplitudes that stay bounded. Every agent sees the same floats and the same next-step prediction target. No evaluator metadata travels with the feed. Phase two runs four explorer architectures — sparse energy keep, a coarse-to-fine pyramid, local-to-global patch encoding, and bounded step-size adaptation — against a flat random-keep control. Phase three translates whatever they invented into property scores after the fact. Phase four repeats the same scorers on a held-out, noisier landscape so a single lucky dataset cannot crown the story.

Beside the fixtures sits a curated literature and data scan: Shannon’s rate-versus-fidelity trade, Mallat’s multiresolution decompositions, predictive coding in cortex models, renormalization as a scale grammar, sparse coding that emerges without being told the dictionary, and representation-learning surveys that treat the search itself as the object. That scan is a map of available peers for protocol design. The bibliography is intentionally short; the physics of the universe is left where it was.

What we hypothesized

If certain organizations of information are simply cheaper to carry when the job is prediction under budget, then independent optimizers should rediscover aspects of those organizations without being baptized into a vocabulary. The water analogy is everyday framing only: streams do not need a lecture on optimal drainage to carve a channel. Gradient becomes path; path becomes channel. In information terms, many independent agents may fall toward similar attractors when the cost surface is shared.

A miniature precursor path — named AHOIS in the paper — already sketches the local version: conventional representation, apparent nuisance, alternative representation, hidden information, useful encoding, adaptive measurement. The Agentic Convergence Experiment asks whether that miniature generalizes when the agents do not share a classroom.

What the fixture suite showed

On the locked synthetic landscapes, the explorer set cleared a pre-registered margin against the flat control on mean structural property scores, and the margin remained above a lower portability gate when the seed and noise changed. That is a real, regenerable result at the fixture tier: independent architectures inventing different intermediate paths still score higher, after translation, on multiscale, hierarchical, local-global, and bounded-adaptation properties than random retention.

Qualifications matter as much as the pass marks. These agents are deterministic optimizers in a research suite, not live multi-lab LLM crews with different employers. Clearing a fixture gate leaves open whether production coding assistants would rediscover nested encodings without instruction. The literature pointers motivate the design; they leave room for a fuller review later. And success here keeps the paper as an application companion until a stricter held-out INCLUDE gate is earned the hard way.

Why the framework still earns a seat at the table

Even a careful mixed or qualified win helps people who actually ship systems. It separates “we taught the model our poetry” from “the cost of useful prediction pushed several searchers toward related encodings.” That separation is useful when a team is deciding whether nested compression, hierarchical features, or bounded adaptation belong in a product because the data demand them — not because a whitepaper used pretty words. It also gives outsiders a door: rerun the pipeline, change the landscape seed, tighten the margins, or refuse the claim if the control catches up.

For Infinite Octaves readers, the invitation is reciprocal. The engine pin stays where it is. This experiment is a test bench that can connect catalog grammar to something empirical without requiring prior conversion. If later live-agent lanes (BYOK, multiple providers, human research-team diversity) fail to rhyme with the fixture attractor, the honest move is to say so and keep the companion off the shelf.

Try the board yourself

The full protocol, gates, and honesty tables live in the whitepaper. The suite under research/synthobs-agentic-convergence-experiment/ regenerates the scoreboard with one npm script, and the standalone repository FractiAI/synthobs-agentic-convergence-experiment carries the same fixtures for external forks. If you care about jobs, rent, and kids in a world where recursive tools keep rewriting their own notes, the practical ask is small: stop testing whether a favored word appears in the answer, and start testing whether independent searchers, under a shared least-cost prediction job, keep inventing the same kinds of cheap structure.
