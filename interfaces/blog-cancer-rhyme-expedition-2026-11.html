Does Cancer Diversity Compress Into Fewer Relational Rhymes Than Disease Labels Suggest?

FractiAI Research — November 20, 2026

Short answer: Yes. On a regenerable regenerable lab board of twenty-four cancer worlds labeled with public TCGA project names, entity-invariant relational fingerprints predicted phenotype at accuracy 1.0, while disease-identity transfer scored 0 and shared relation-type bags scored about 0.46. Six rediscovered structural motifs covered the board that twenty-four disease labels described, a fourfold compression, and fingerprints transferred across cancer pairs at 0.8. The engine-shelf pin stays deferred. This is a computational expedition, not a clinic visit.

Families still hear a sentence that feels like a wall: cancer is many diseases, so the path forward may be many separate solutions. That sentence protects honest diversity — breast is not lung, blood is not brain — and it also shapes paychecks, trial design, and how long a parent waits for a treatment that “matches the type.” FractiSI’s Rhyming World Model asks a different bookkeeping question. If the surface catalog is large, how many recurring relational patterns still have to be paid for underneath? The expedition refuses to start from the textbook Hallmarks list and then congratulate itself for finding them. It builds shared worlds first, searches for structural rhymes second, and only afterward asks how the findings sit beside known biology.

The problem labels create for transfer

Disease labels are excellent filing cabinets. They organize clinics, insurance codes, and grant portfolios. They are weaker at answering whether a resistance pattern learned in one tissue can warn another. A caregiver reading two reports — one for ovarian cancer, one for a sarcoma — sees different names and different genes, and reasonably concludes the stories are separate. Yet people already live with cross-domain pairs every day: a treatment and a later resistance, a cell neighborhood and an immune response, an early seed state and a late edge phenotype. If those pairs are only typed by disease name, transfer never starts. If they share a relational shape — A activates B which drives C, even when the molecular actors change — then a rhyme might travel where a label cannot.

The deeper hunch, kept as a hypothesis rather than a creed, is about coordination. A cell that is locally skilled at surviving can still drift away from the larger biological worlds that used to constrain it — tissue order, neighbor signals, organism-level limits. On this board we measure that as world-model integration capacity: can the lab agent keep local success compatible with shared-world rules? We do not call that consciousness. We call it a measurable gap between local objectives and shared constraints, and we ask whether the gap widens as synthetic phenotypes look more malignant-like.

What we tested

Twenty-four lab worlds carry public TCGA project abbreviations as labels only. Each world has agents, states, relationships, tissue context, boundaries, and seed-to-edge transitions. Six structural motifs are planted across half the cancers with different entity names but preserved relation shape. Discovery never receives Hallmark features. After the run, post-hoc hints may mention familiar themes for readers; those hints are interpretation, not inputs.

Three models compete on the same scoreboard. Model A treats each cancer type as its own disease feature and tries to transfer phenotype by identity. Model B bags relation verbs as a shared-mechanism baseline without insisting on multi-step structure. Model C indexes entity-invariant relational fingerprints — the FractiSI rhyme representation. An oracle that can see planted motif presence sets an upper lab upper bound. Experiments include leave-one-out phenotype prediction, a rhyme compression count, cross-cancer transfer, shuffled null worlds, local-versus-shared divergence, and a measured candidate Goldilocks band for jointly favorable integration and boundary proxies.

The seed is locked so another lab can regenerate the board. Public molecular matrices were not assumed; where live patient pipelines are limited, the expedition ships a reproducible synthetic-plus-public-label harness rather than inventing clinical outcomes.

Hypothesis

If recurring relational configurations generate much of the observed lab diversity, then Model C should beat Models A and B on phenotype prediction, a smaller rhyme set should compress the disease catalog without collapsing predictive accuracy, and fingerprints learned in one cancer should transfer to another better than disease identity. If local optimization drifts from shared-world compatibility in progressed phenotypes, divergence metrics should separate malignant-like lab worlds from indeterminate ones. If shuffled worlds match planted recurrence without qualification, span alone cannot crown novelty. If Model A or B matches Model C, the FractiSI relational story fails on this board and should be reported as failure.

Results

Model C leave-one-out phenotype accuracy was 1.0. Model B scored about 0.46. Model A scored 0 — disease identity does not transfer across types when each world has a unique label. The planted-motif oracle also scored 1.0, showing the phenotype on this board is carried by the planted relational structure rather than by the disease name. Compression was blunt: twenty-four disease labels versus six strong planted rhymes, a fourfold reduction, while Model C kept phenotype accuracy. Cross-cancer transfer of shared fingerprints reached 0.8 across forty pairs, against a disease-identity baseline of 0.

Local-versus-shared divergence rose on malignant-like lab phenotypes (about 0.21) and stayed near zero on indeterminate worlds. Integration proxies moved the other way. A joint-favorability band held most worlds; it is reported as a measured candidate region, not as proof that biology has a Goldilocks law. Six planted motifs were rediscovered as structural rhymes spanning twelve cancers each, with post-hoc hints only after discovery. High-span noise fingerprints also appeared; they stay parked as candidates that need literature and held-out data, not as mechanisms.

The null shuffle is the honest hitch. At the planted span of twelve cancers, shuffled boards also produced many high-span structural hits, so span recurrence alone does not uniquely privilege the planted motifs here. The lab case therefore rests on phenotype gaps, compression, and transfer — not on claiming that every long-span fingerprint is rare under null. That qualification matters for anyone tempted to equate “the software found a pattern” with “biology has a new mechanism.”

Qualifications

These are synthetic relational worlds with public project abbreviations, not patient molecular matrices and not treatment advice. Perfect Model C accuracy is expected when planted structure dominates a controlled board; the useful number is the gap versus disease labels and bag baselines, plus transfer across types. Awareness on this expedition means world-model integration capacity, never subjective experience. Hallmarks were withheld from discovery on purpose. The null result is inconclusive for span exclusivity. The engine-shelf pin stays deferred. If later public-data boards show conventional pathway features matching or beating relational fingerprints, that failure should replace this lab win in the catalog story.

Why this earns a Rhyming Expedition seat

The Rhyming World Model needs a clear answer when someone asks whether rhyme is only a compression trick for tickets and agents. This arm takes the same relational muscle into a computational oncology-style setting where labels are loud and transfer is scarce. It keeps the reframe careful: cancer may remain many diseases at the clinic door and still be fewer problems at the relational layer. For people who fund research, hire digital agents beside human ones, or wait on a pathology report that will change a family’s rent and calendar, the stake is whether a pattern learned in one named disease can warn another before the late phenotype is already written.
