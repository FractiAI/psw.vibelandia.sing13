Should Computation Become the Primary Workhorse of Science?

FractiAI Research — October 13, 2026

Grant season turns every lab into an accountant. Paychecks and rent do not care whether the breakthrough came from a GPU cluster or a cold room; they care whether validated progress arrived before the calendar year closed. The Homeostasis Expedition manuscript frames that trade as a falsifiable question: for one fully costed researcher-year, does computation-first work produce more independently validated discoveries and more implemented, measurable outcomes per dollar than wet-laboratory-first work — and does a Discovery-to-Implementation Pipeline earn its overhead? We prespecified a four-condition factorial, then executed CW-2, a synthetic event simulation with seed 20261013 and 200 matched repetitions. The run populates Appendix B with numerical means. Those numbers are useful for stress-testing assumptions; they are not a certificate that national science policy should reroute bench funding tomorrow.

Method: one human-year, four arms, hard stop at day 365

Condition A is human-led computation-first research without a translation pipeline. B adds the pipeline. C is wet-lab-first without the pipeline. D combines wet-lab-first with the pipeline. Each repetition draws a matched portfolio of twenty-four projects and walks the same milestone grammar: candidate, substantive claim, independent validation, funding delay, implementation, and measurable progress, with explicit tallies for work still unfinished when the clock hits day 365.

Costs roll up across researcher time, compute, data, facilities, equipment, materials, validation, failed work, implementation, pipeline operations, and overhead. The simulator uses declared time and dollar parameters — faster candidate cycles and lower materials spend in computation-first arms, higher facility and reagent burdens in wet-lab arms — rather than calibrating every coefficient to NIH or industry ledgers.

Hypothesis under test

The primary contrast is environment: computation-first versus wet-lab-first holding pipeline status fixed. The secondary contrast is pipeline on versus off within each environment. A conservative decision rule would favor computation as the “primary workhorse” only if it improves quality-adjusted validated output or lowers fully allocated cost for an equivalent endpoint without unacceptable integrity losses. The pipeline would be justified only if incremental measurable outcomes exceed incremental spend by a margin policy makers accept.

What CW-2 reported (synthetic means)

Under the declared model, mean validated discoveries per researcher-year were 2.98 (A), 3.55 (B), 2.31 (C), and 2.83 (D). Mean measurable outcomes were 1.44, 2.51, 1.04, and 1.80. Total expenditure means clustered near $145k for A, $194k for B, $290k for C, and $375k for D — wet-lab arms spent more in this parameterization, while computation-first arms moved more projects through validation and measurement.

Adding the pipeline increased measurable outcomes in both environments (roughly +1.07 for computation, +0.76 for wet-lab) while raising total spend. Unfinished projects at day 365 were more common in wet-lab no-pipeline runs (mean ~10.7 for C) than in computation pipeline runs (~1.1 for B), suggesting calendar bottlenecks differ by arm — but that pattern is an artifact of the timing assumptions, not a measured property of real core facilities.

The integrity row is mixed rather than triumphant. Mean false accepts were higher in computation-first arms (~0.22–0.26 per year) than in wet-lab arms (~0.04–0.02) under the validation sensitivity parameters we coded. Productivity and error rates moved in opposite directions. Any policy story that counts only throughput would misread the ledger.

How to read the framework without over-reading the numbers

The value of the exercise is structural. It forces candidate, validated, implemented, and measurable states to stay separate; it keeps failed work and unfinished projects visible; it prices pipeline overhead instead of hiding translation in “support” lines. That is the same discipline a principal investigator feels when a promising computational hit still needs a confirmatory assay, or when a beautiful gel does not survive blind replication.

For families juggling job boards and rent, the analogy is blunt: the cheaper experiment is not cheaper if it never survives the repeat trial that unlocks the next paycheck. Science at scale faces the same arithmetic, which is why the manuscript refuses to collapse discovery environment and translation pipeline into a single buzzword.

Invitation to replicate and calibrate

The full paper lists event rules, Appendix B tables, factorial contrasts, and an evidence ledger that separates design-established claims from synthetic simulation results. Reproduce the run via suite research/synthobs-computation-workhorse-science/ (npm run research:synthobs-computation-workhorse-science) or the standalone repository; inspect data/cw2_summary.json for the seed-20261013 summary object. Independent teams can swap validation sensitivities, wage assumptions, or portfolio sizes and watch which conclusions survive — that sensitivity work is the path from synthetic illustration to evidence anyone would bet a grant program on.
