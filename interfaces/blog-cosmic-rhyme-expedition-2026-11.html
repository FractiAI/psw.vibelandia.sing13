What We Found Looking at the Sky Through a Protein-Folding Lens

FractiAI Research — November 17, 2026

A paycheck ledger can look tidy as a list of deposits and still hide the relationship that decides whether rent clears: which week the deposit lands against which week the landlord drafts. Sky catalogs have the same habit. They are published as rows — planet, encounter, galaxy, multipole — and the interesting structure often lives in who couples to whom. The Cosmic Rhyme Expedition aimed the protein-folding method at those public rows and then asked two sharper follow-ups: after you remove how the catalogs were built, is anything left that looks like candidate physics? And when you pack the same rows as language the way earlier expeditions packed proteins and documents for language models, does the folding rhyme still show up?

Bottom line before the jargon

We found four things, and the order matters. First, the method travels: on exoplanets, near-Earth close approaches, deep-sky objects, and the WMAP microwave spectrum, a relational board recovered partners that a local linear window and a flat dump missed. Second, an open search — not told to hunt filaments or dark matter — kept catalog fingerprints after held-out checks and scrambled-number nulls. Third, because those first arms were representation and survey history rather than new physics, we added a residual search: remove selection axes and continuum templates, then test leftovers. Two WMAP residual lags cleared that bar as candidates under the protocol. Fourth, the same LINEAR / FLAT / AWARE grammar still wins when the materials are language packs instead of feature boards — the LLM rhyme inside cosmic data — without needing a frontier API key for the default receipt.

How novel is each finding?

Most novel for this program: the folding rhyme is not stuck in proteins or office datasets. The same grammar works on real NASA, JPL, OpenNGC, and WMAP fixtures anyone can re-download, both as measured couplings and as E9–E11-style language packs. That is methodological novelty — proof the optics still focus when the material is the sky’s public catalogs.

Moderately novel as observation: unsupervised motifs that survived held-out slices and feature-permutation nulls. We did not type “find selection effects” into the pipeline. What lived looks like survey history and catalog geometry. Working astronomers already know surveys are biased. What is new in this receipt is that a protein-style relationship board recovered those fingerprints without being handed the label — useful for anyone who has ever cleaned a cross-agency table between shifts and wondered why the “same” file keeps teaching the same wrong lesson when read only top to bottom.

Candidate physics, said carefully: C1–C3 were never a novel-physics claim. C4 residualizes discovery year and distance on exoplanets, smooth continuum on WMAP, and related selection axes elsewhere, then asks whether leftover couplings still beat shuffle and footprint nulls. Two residual lags on the WMAP temperature spectrum — lags 11 and 14 — cleared that gate and sit off the locked acoustic-spacing template. We call them candidate novel physics under this protocol. We do not call them a confirmed new law. Beam, mask, and instrument explanations remain open. Lags near 17–19 lean acoustic and are labeled rediscovery; exoplanet mass–radius and period–radius residuals rediscover known population templates.

LLM rhyme without a bake-off: C5 packs each partner-recovery task as LINEAR (local window text), FLAT (unstructured dump), and AWARE (leading candidate plus ranked relational board) — the same packing shape used when proteins, Hotpot bridge titles, and FRED series were shown to language models. A pack-compliant reader that follows those instructions recovers the gold partner at 100% on all four layers while LINEAR and FLAT stay near zero. A lexical-blind reader that ignores the board and only overlaps tokens with the query line fails on AWARE. Live model calls are optional via environment keys; the default expedition signal does not depend on them.

What the searches actually said out loud

Exoplanets. Discovery year keeps company with host-star size and temperature — a fingerprint of human observing history sitting inside the public file. After residualizing year and distance, mass–radius and stellar mass–radius couplings come back as known-template rediscoveries, which is exactly what a residual physics arm should do when the leftover structure is familiar.

Deep-sky catalog. Apparent size keeps company with sky position and listed brightness among the strongest couplings. We audit those as selection and morphology candidates. We do not get to rename them “filament discovery” after the fact.

Microwave spectrum. Multipoles have lag partners; a lag board and a language pack both recover them when a tiny local window does not. Residual lags 11 and 14 are the physics-search candidates; nearby acoustic-leaning lags are rediscoveries.

Language packs. The AWARE pack is shorter on average than the flat dump in our exoplanet sample and still carries the ranked board. That is the same token-economics intuition from earlier folding-rhyme work: structure in the prompt beats stuffing more unordered text.

How we looked, without romance

LINEAR reads a local strip of the catalog, the way people skim a spreadsheet. FLAT dumps more of the same material without ranking. AWARE builds an explicit board of measured couplings from the same continuous fields. Partner-recovery tests ask whether you can name a held-out long-range partner. Motif search asks which recurring glues survive when half the rows are held out and when the numbers are scrambled. Residual physics search asks what still couples after known selection is regressed out. Language-pack rhyme asks whether those same partners survive when the materials are sentences instead of matrices. One npm script regenerates the receipt. No account. No wallet. That matters for people who cannot book telescope time but can still keep an argument honest on a laptop after the kids are asleep.

Why a careful novelty ladder still earns a seat

Science that only ships fireworks eventually trains readers to ignore the quiet results that pay the rent. A method that generalizes from proteins to knowledge to macro series to public sky files — then admits when its first finds are catalog fingerprints, then actually runs a residual physics search, then checks whether the rhyme still holds as language — is the kind of result a careful lab should want. Teachers, analysts, and researchers stretched between paycheck and curiosity need the same discipline: change the representation, hold the materials still, let held-out data decide, and say plainly what is new, what is candidate, and what is not.

Read the whitepaper for gates and tables. Rerun the suite. Bring a sharper audit label if you have one. The next useful fight is not whether relationships matter in the abstract. It is which residual candidates and which language-pack behaviors still look like news when someone else holds the same public files.
