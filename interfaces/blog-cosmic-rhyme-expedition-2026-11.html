What We Found Looking at the Sky Through a Protein-Folding Lens

FractiAI Research — November 17, 2026

People ask the physics question first, and they should. Did anything look like new physics in the public sky files? After we removed how catalogs are built and smoothed the known microwave continuum, two leftover patterns on the WMAP temperature spectrum — lags 11 and 14 — still beat our null tests. We call them candidate novel physics under this protocol. We do not call them a confirmed new force, particle, or law. Beam, mask, and instrument stories remain open. The folding method travels, open search finds catalog fingerprints, and the language-pack rhyme now lives in its own expedition that also peeks at the human genome.

Any novel physics, in kitchen English?

Maybe — as candidates, not as settled discovery. The first three arms of the expedition were never a physics claim. They showed that a relationship board recovers partners a scroll-through misses, and that an open search mostly surfaces survey history: when planets were discovered lines up with what kinds of host stars surveys were watching; how big deep-sky objects look lines up with where they sit and how bright they are listed. That is useful for anyone who has cleaned a cross-agency table between shifts. It is not a new landlord for the universe.

So we added a residual search. Take discovery year and distance out of the exoplanet couplings. Take a smooth continuum out of the microwave spectrum. Then ask what still couples, and whether those leftovers beat shuffled-number and footprint nulls. Two WMAP residual lags cleared that bar and sit off the locked acoustic-spacing template. Nearby lags that lean acoustic, and exoplanet mass–radius leftovers, came back labeled as known rediscoveries — which is what a careful search should do when the leftover structure is familiar.

What else this expedition found

The folding rhyme travels. On NASA exoplanets, JPL close approaches, OpenNGC deep-sky objects, and the WMAP microwave spectrum, organizing the same numbers as relationships recovered held-out long-range partners that a local linear window and a flat dump missed — roughly zero percent under the weak reads, full recovery under the relationship board on our long-range tests.

Open motif search, not told to hunt filaments or dark matter, kept eight patterns after held-out checks and feature-scramble nulls. The clearest ones are fingerprints of how the observable cosmos is recorded, not renamed cosmic-web detections.

What the residual search returned, in one breath

Two WMAP residual lags — eleven and fourteen — beat nulls after continuum removal and sit off the locked acoustic-spacing template. We keep calling them candidates. Seven known-template rediscoveries also returned, including acoustic-leaning lags near seventeen through nineteen and exoplanet mass–radius couplings after year and distance were removed. A sieve that only finds fireworks is broken. A sieve that also rediscovers the familiar is doing its job. Anyone who has audited a family budget after a surprise bill knows that feeling: the leftover that still does not fit is interesting, and the leftovers that match known categories are how you trust the spreadsheet.

How we looked, without romance

LINEAR skims a local strip the way people skim a spreadsheet. FLAT dumps more of the same material without ranking. AWARE builds an explicit board of measured couplings from the same continuous fields. Motif search asks which recurring glues survive held-out slices and scrambled numbers. Residual physics search asks what still couples after known selection is removed. One npm script regenerates the receipt. No account. No wallet. That matters for people who cannot book telescope time but can still keep an argument honest on a laptop after the kids are asleep.

Why a careful ladder still earns a seat

Science that only ships fireworks trains readers to ignore quiet results that pay the rent. A method that generalizes from proteins to public sky files — then admits when first finds are catalog fingerprints, then actually searches residuals for candidate physics, then points language packing to its own receipt — is the kind of result a careful lab should want. Teachers, analysts, and researchers stretched between paycheck and curiosity need the same discipline: change the representation, hold the materials still, let held-out data decide, and say plainly what is new, what is candidate, and what is not.
