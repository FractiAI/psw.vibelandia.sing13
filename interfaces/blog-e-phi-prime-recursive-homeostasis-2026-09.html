Research note · Recursive homeostasis

When Change, Proportion, and Boundaries Share One Loop, Does Evolution Stay Contained?

FractiAI Research — September 28, 2026

A paycheck that never updates when rent rises is not stability; it is stagnation with a polite face. A budget that rewrites every line every week until the rent number itself has wandered away is not adaptation; it is loss of identity. Between those failures sits a harder question for recursive systems: can a process keep changing while still recognizing itself? The earlier EGS Recursive Fidelity Test asked whether one nest constant — including the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.618 — could reduce cumulative drift on its own. The live answer was clear enough to publish: nothing to crown. Dynamic switching among constants also failed to beat a simple fixed policy. This note is the next expedition, not a rewrite of that baseline. It asks whether a single unified architecture that combines continuous change, proportional structure, and discrete containment can produce useful evolution that stays bounded — and, after two diagnostic forks, whether measuring irreversible loss instead of distance from the starting state changes the verdict.

What the live run found

Observed answer — nothing crowned across three locked layers. There is no combination of constants to install as a winner. Not e alone. Not φ alone. Not primes alone. Not the product e×φ×prime under open-loop sequencing, matched-budget regulation, or the newest adaptive coupled fork. Engine shelf stays off. What changed is the quality of the failure: each layer removed a confound the previous layer still carried.

V1 (locked). Ordinary recursion (Control A) spent about 82% of generations inside the Goldilocks band because it barely moved. The unified stack that was forced to transform, restructure, and contain every generation scored only ~23%. Evolution efficiency E/(D+ε) already showed the asymmetry: Control A ~0.044 · Control B ~0.054 · Unified ~0.028. Ablating open-loop φ nearly recovered ordinary Goldilocks rates — the proportional stage was overdriving, not “φ is false as an idea.”

EPH-RH-D (locked). Equalizing path length and treating φ as a regulator flipped the story versus Control A: regulator+prime posted higher useful evolution and higher E/(D+ε) (~0.029 vs ~0.011) with slightly lower bleed. Matched non-prime containment with the same regulator was still more efficient (~0.034) than prime (~0.029). Random novelty led the board. Homeostatic signature rate was 0. Control A still under-spent the Q target — a bookkeeping hole the next fork closed.

EPH-RH-D2 (live engine gate) — fairer board, still withhold. Every arm now terminates at exact transformation budget Q_max = 2.4. Success uses loss-aware fidelity F = (E·R·I)/(L+λB+ε), where L is irreversible information loss and I is structural-invariant identity — not distance from the original series. Coupled adaptive + prime beats Control A on F (~2.85 vs ~1.84) and beats matched non-prime coupled (~1.95). Bleed and irreversible loss both fall versus Control A. That is real progress on the audit’s metric and budget complaints. The gate still fails on the two checks that matter for the rewritten hypothesis: sequential pipeline G scores higher F than coupled interaction H (~3.07 vs ~2.85), and open-loop φ still beats adaptive φ on both F and L. Random still leads F (~3.35). New-attractor homeostasis after perturbation remains at rate 0. So: do not crown e, φ, primes, or their product; do treat exact-Q + loss-aware F as the honest scoreboard going forward.

The problem after “nothing to crown”

Recursive pipelines — summarizers that rewrite their own summaries, policies that revise policies, household tools that “helpfully” reorganize last month’s categories — fail in two everyday ways. They freeze: the rent line never moves, the job description never absorbs a new duty, the model never admits the world changed. Or they dissolve: each rewrite is locally plausible, yet after twenty passes the document no longer matches what legal approved, what payroll can pay, or what a parent can explain to a child. Engineers often measure one-step error and miss the slope. ERFT measured cumulative drift under matched recursion and refused a magic-number story. That refusal does not end the design problem. It relocates it. If one constant is not enough, and if switching constants is not enough, is there a structured loop in which different mathematical roles share one generation?

Everyday life already suggests a three-part intuition without proving it. Continuous change looks a little like compound growth — the mathematics associated with e. Recognizable proportion under nesting looks a little like self-similar split — the mathematics associated with φ. Containment looks a little like a sealed jar whose size does not factor into smaller integer lids — the mathematics associated with primes. Those are metaphors until an experiment forces them to compete with ordinary smoothing and with a randomized look-alike that has the same number of knobs.

The unified experiment and the diagnostic fork

The architecture under test began as one sequential system: each generation applies an e-like continuous transform, then a φ-like proportional structuring, then a prime-defined containment step. The question was never whether drift can be driven to zero. Zero can be stagnation. The question was whether the combined loop can sit in a Goldilocks band: change above a low floor, below a collapse ceiling, with bleed low and useful evolution positive.

Senior audit of that first board found several traps that can suppress the phenomenon you hoped to see. Judging success by distance from the origin penalizes legitimate transformation. A maximum-step cap is not a conserved transformation budget. Sequential P∘Φ∘E is not the same claim as compositional e×φ×prime. Open-loop φ strength that does not respond to state is a controller parameter, not a proportional regulator. Novelty without identity preservation lets random architectures inflate “useful evolution.” Homeostasis need not return to the starting state; it can settle on a new attractor after adaptive correction.

EPH-RH-D2 implements those corrections without rewriting V1 or D. Every arm spends exactly Q_max. Stepwise displacement is separated from invariant identity. Irreversible loss L replaces origin drift in the denominator of F. Adaptive φ responds to the local regime. Coupled arms add an interaction term beside the sequential pipeline. Perturbation tests ask for correction to a new stable attractor, not a march home to X₀.

Hypothesis and falsifiers

The rewritten hypothesis under D2 is stronger and more falsifiable: homeostasis equals controlled transformation plus adaptive proportional regulation plus bounded compartmentalization, producing meaningful change with low irreversible loss, low bleed, and high recoverability. The experiment must be able to fail — and on the live board it does, in specific places. Coupled interaction may lose to a simple pipeline. Adaptive φ may lose to open-loop φ. Random novelty may still dominate F. A new attractor may never form after perturbation. Those outcomes are first-class results. They are not temporary embarrassments waiting for a rescue narrative that rewrites the locked V1 and D nulls.

Why the framing still helps even when results are mixed

Methodologically, this expedition keeps scientific questions from being smuggled into each other. ERFT asked whether a numerical constant alone moves cumulative drift. A grammar lane asked whether nest algebra helps when every arm receives the same operator. A dynamic lane asked whether switching among constants beats the best fixed policy. This paper asks whether a compositional architecture with distinct candidate roles can produce a bounded evolutionary regime — and whether fixing the measurement of loss, budget, and coupling changes the answer. Separating the questions is the point. It prevents a favorite constant from being smuggled back in as “the combination secretly meant φ all along,” and it prevents a pretty story about primes from being mistaken for zero-bleed physics.

That posture matters outside the lab. People who ship recursive tools for jobs, rent, and kids need a vocabulary that can say “we changed on purpose” without saying “we leaked identity.” Distinguishing drift from bleed, and displacement from irreversible loss, is that vocabulary. Distinguishing useful evolution from raw novelty is how you refuse to congratulate a system for wandering farther from payroll truth.

What comes next without rewriting the baseline

ERFT-V5, ERFT-D, V1 Goldilocks null, and EPH-RH-D matched-budget null stay locked. This expedition does not reopen them as “φ won after all.” The live D2 fork does not become a quiet rescue narrative either. Exact-Q and loss-aware F made the board fairer and finally gave prime structure an F edge over matched non-prime under the same coupling — but sequential still beat coupled, adaptive still lost to open-loop, and homeostasis as new-attractor recovery is not shown. Engine pin stays off. The honest next step, if any, is confirmatory work on held-out fixtures with D2 metrics frozen before looking — targeting the two failed checks (interaction coefficients; adaptive φ law) — or a clear stop if those checks repeatedly fail. Either way, the invitation is practical: read the whitepaper, inspect the three scoreboards, and rerun the pipeline from the standalone repository. Change the operators, hold the evaluator still, and see whether your domain shows evolution without dissolution — or another clean, publishable no.
