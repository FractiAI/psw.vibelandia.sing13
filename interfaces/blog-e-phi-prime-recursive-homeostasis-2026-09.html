Research note · Recursive homeostasis

When Change, Proportion, and Boundaries Share One Loop, Does Evolution Stay Contained?

FractiAI Research — September 29, 2026

A paycheck that never updates when rent rises is not stability; it is stagnation with a polite face. A budget that rewrites every line every week until the rent number itself has wandered away is not adaptation; it is loss of identity. Between those failures sits a harder question for recursive systems: can a process keep changing while still recognizing itself? The earlier EGS Recursive Fidelity Test asked whether one nest constant — including the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.618 — could reduce cumulative drift on its own. The live answer was clear enough to publish: nothing to crown. Dynamic switching among constants also failed to beat a simple fixed policy. This note is the next expedition, not a rewrite of that baseline. It asked whether a unified architecture combining continuous change, proportional structure, and discrete containment can produce useful evolution that stays bounded — and, after a sequence of diagnostic forks, whether the missing move is selective reconciliation of novelty against an immutable canonical rather than another retune of the change operator itself.

What the live run found

Observed answer — six layers deep, with a mechanism signal that finally moves three axes the same way, and a gate that was rejecting the interesting case. There is still no combination of constants to install as a winner. Not e alone. Not φ alone. Not primes alone. Not the product as a dynamical controller, and not the same product as an information architecture under matched storage budgets. What changed is sharper: lossless identity retention (E2) simultaneously improved recoverability, useful evolution, and bleed versus ordinary recursion — the first coherent Goldilocks-direction signal in this sequence — while verify→commit (E4) protected identity by refusing most of the interesting transforms. The newest fork freezes e and E2 and asks whether selective reconciliation can keep that direction while restoring useful commitment.

V1 (locked). Ordinary recursion (Control A) spent about 82% of generations inside the Goldilocks band because it barely moved. The unified stack that was forced to transform, restructure, and contain every generation scored only ~23%. Evolution efficiency E/(D+ε) already showed the asymmetry: Control A ~0.044 · Control B ~0.054 · Unified ~0.028. Ablating open-loop φ nearly recovered ordinary Goldilocks rates — the proportional stage was overdriving, not “φ is false as an idea.”

EPH-RH-D (locked). Equalizing path length and treating φ as a regulator flipped the story versus Control A: regulator+prime posted higher useful evolution and higher E/(D+ε) (~0.029 vs ~0.011) with slightly lower bleed. Matched non-prime containment with the same regulator was still more efficient (~0.034) than prime (~0.029). Random novelty led the board. Homeostatic signature rate was 0.

EPH-RH-D2 (locked). Exact transformation budget Q_max = 2.4 and loss-aware fidelity F = (E·R·I)/(L+λB+ε) made the dynamical board fair. Coupled adaptive + prime beat Control A and matched non-prime on F — but sequential pipeline still beat coupled interaction, open-loop φ still beat adaptive φ, random still led F, and new-attractor homeostasis stayed at rate 0.

EPH-IA (locked withhold) — information ledger. Prior layers tested constants as transformation operators when the deeper hypothesis is that they organize information. EPH-IA runs an encode → store → transform → retrieve → reconstruct ledger with matched representation budgets (84 slots). Live IA board: φ hierarchy alone leads IFE (~1.381). Every arm that includes the e-update kernel loses recursive conservation (RCR falls from ~0.72 to ~0.61). Full stack IFE ~1.15 — below A, below best single (φ), below best pair (φ+prime). Complementarity fails. Gate false. Locked.

EPH-IA-R (locked withhold) — e reversibility + dual-state. Every architecture containing the present e-update collapses into the same lower RCR regime. IAR freezes the IA board and runs e-forward, exact inverse, lossless identity-retain, explore-only with canonical untouched, and Transform → Organize → Contain → Verify → Commit. Live IAR board: e-forward RCR ~0.611 versus Control A ~0.720. Exact inverse restores RCR (~0.721). Lossless identity-retain (E2) leads RCR (~0.750), useful evolution (~0.021), and efficiency E/(L+ε) (~0.082) while lowering bleed (~0.290 vs ~0.308) — three desirable directions at once. Dual-state verify→commit (E4) beats e-forward on RCR (~0.711) with raw commit ~0.24, but collapses useful E (~0.003) because rejected explores count as zero committed evolution. IAR gate false. Locked. E2 frozen as the mechanism signal.

EPH-IA-Δ (live engine gate) — selective reconciliation beside frozen e. The next question is not how to retune e. It is whether the system can selectively preserve information rather than destroying it or rejecting the transformation. Architecture under test: Canonical → e-Explore → φ-Organize → p-Contain → Δ-Reconcile → Verify → Commit. The canonical stays immutable during exploration. Frozen e value-deltas proved recoverability-destructive under simple α-blends, so ρ repair adopts explore structure (addresses from organize/contain) while keeping canonical values unless a slot-wise update does not lower recoverability. Novelty is journaled E2-style (explore delta × identity recoverability). A Goldilocks hit requires three axes at once — RCR above A, bleed below A, and useful evolution above A. Useful Commit Rate counts only commits that clear all three axes.

Live Δ board: Control A RCR ~0.723 · E ~0.017 · bleed ~0.305. E2 freeze still clears three-axis (RCR ~0.750 · E ~0.021 · bleed ~0.290). E4 remains over-conservative (RCR ~0.656 · E ~0.006 · useful commit ~0.15). Δ-reconcile (R) matches E2 on the three axes (RCR ~0.750 · E ~0.021 · bleed ~0.290) with useful commit rate 0.50. IAD gate: true. Engine shelf: INCLUDE on these fixtures — a mechanism signal for bounded recursive evolution, not a finished physics crown. Held-out confirmation still required.

The problem after “nothing to crown”

Recursive pipelines — summarizers that rewrite their own summaries, policies that revise policies, household tools that “helpfully” reorganize last month’s categories — fail in two everyday ways. They freeze: the rent line never moves, the job description never absorbs a new duty, the model never admits the world changed. Or they dissolve: each rewrite is locally plausible, yet after twenty passes the document no longer matches what legal approved, what payroll can pay, or what a parent can explain to a child. Engineers often measure one-step error and miss the slope. ERFT measured cumulative drift under matched recursion and refused a magic-number story. That refusal does not end the design problem. It relocates it.

The dynamical forks asked whether e, φ, and primes could steer a changing state without dissolving it. Those boards taught useful engineering lessons — Goldilocks hit-rate can reward stagnation; origin distance is the wrong denominator for legitimate change; exact budgets matter; open-loop φ can overdrive — but they never measured storage efficiency, retrieval precision, or reconstruction fidelity. When the information ledger then showed that e destroys recoverability, the natural trap was either to abandon the change operator or to gate commits so tightly that almost nothing lands. E2 and E4 together name that trap: protect identity by journaling novelty you never commit, or protect identity by rejecting most explores. Neither is the Goldilocks signature the expedition set out to find — nonzero useful change, high recoverability, and low bleed at once.

The unified experiment and the mechanism test

The architecture under test began as one sequential dynamical system: each generation applies an e-like continuous transform, then a φ-like proportional structuring, then a prime-defined containment step. Senior audit of that board, and of the matched-budget and loss-aware forks that followed, found a category error: the experiment was testing controllers, not information architecture. EPH-IA does not rewrite those nulls. It adds a mechanism test beside them. IAR then isolates e’s loss channel. EPH-IA-Δ freezes that e kernel and freezes E2’s three-axis signal, then inserts a reconciliation operator — not a new mathematical constant, a functional repair step that asks which portion of the explored delta is genuinely new and recoverable.

On the ledger, every arm receives the same slot budget. Encode builds a hierarchical block pyramid. Store keeps the slots. Transform applies either a plain mild update or an e-derived continuous EMA. Retrieve pulls by address. Reconstruct fills coverage from stored blocks. On the Δ board, explore may mutate; reconcile proposes X_n + α·Δ; verify checks three-axis constraints against Control A baselines; commit only when useful novelty survives. The experiment can fail cleanly — and the gate is designed so that stagnation-by-reject no longer looks like success.

Hypothesis and falsifiers

The information-architecture hypothesis said e, φ, and prime structure may be complementary organization primitives. The live IA board falsified that package on these fixtures: φ hierarchy is a mild IFE leader; the e-update kernel costs conservation; primes do not uniquely win IFE; the product is not complementary. IAR then showed that removing the loss channel restores conservation, and that E2 moves RCR, E, and bleed together in the desirable direction. E4 showed that a hard verify→commit gate can protect identity by refusing most interesting transformations — over-conservative evolution. The Δ hypothesis is sharper: selective reconciliation can preserve E2-direction gains while restoring useful commitment, without retuning e. Falsifiers are explicit. If E2’s three-axis lock fails to replicate, stop and re-audit before architecture work. If reconcile clears recoverability only by collapsing useful evolution below A, that is another over-conservative fail. If useful commit rate stays below the floor while raw commit looks healthy, the optimizer found garbage commits or stagnation in disguise.

Why the framing still helps even when results are mixed

Methodologically, this expedition keeps scientific questions from being smuggled into each other. ERFT asked whether a numerical constant alone moves cumulative drift. A dynamic lane asked whether switching among constants beats the best fixed policy. The homeostasis layers asked whether a compositional controller can produce bounded evolution. EPH-IA asks whether the same mathematical structures improve the information lifecycle itself. IAR asks whether e’s information-loss channel is the destroyer. Δ asks whether useful novelty can be extracted without all-or-nothing rewrite. Separating the questions is the point. It prevents a favorite constant from being smuggled back in as “the combination secretly meant φ all along,” and it prevents a pretty story about primes from being mistaken for a database index after you only ever tested a firewall.

That posture matters outside the lab. People who ship recursive tools for jobs, rent, and kids need a vocabulary that can say “we changed on purpose” without saying “we leaked identity,” and a second vocabulary that can say “we kept the rent line’s identity while accepting the new overtime rule” without confusing that claim with “our controller stayed pretty by refusing every update.” Distinguishing destruction, stagnation, and selective repair is how you refuse to congratulate a system for wandering farther from payroll truth — or for filing the truth under the wrong address — or for never updating the address at all.
