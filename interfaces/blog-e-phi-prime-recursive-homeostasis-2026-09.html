When Change, Proportion, and Boundaries Share One Loop, Does Evolution Stay Contained?

FractiAI Research — September 29, 2026

A paycheck that never updates when rent rises is not stability; it is stagnation with a polite face. A budget that rewrites every line every week until the rent number itself has wandered away is not adaptation; it is loss of identity. Between those failures sits a harder question for recursive systems: can a process keep changing while still recognizing itself? The earlier EGS Recursive Fidelity Test asked whether one nest constant — including the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.618 — could reduce cumulative drift on its own. The live answer was clear enough to publish: nothing to crown. Dynamic switching among constants also failed to beat a simple fixed policy. This note is the next expedition, not a rewrite of that baseline. It asked whether a unified architecture combining continuous change, proportional structure, and discrete containment can produce useful evolution that stays bounded — and, after three diagnostic forks, whether the deeper mistake was asking those constants to steer dynamics when the real claim may have been about how information is stored, addressed, and reconstructed.

What the live run found

Observed answer — nothing crowned across five locked layers, with a sharper diagnosis of where the benefit dies. There is no combination of constants to install as a winner. Not e alone. Not φ alone. Not primes alone. Not the product e×φ×prime as a dynamical controller, and not the same product as an information architecture under matched storage budgets. Engine shelf stays off. What changed is the quality of the failure: the information ledger showed that every arm carrying the present e-update collapses recoverability, and the newest fork asked whether that is an e-loss-channel problem rather than a verdict on the whole hypothesis.

V1 (locked). Ordinary recursion (Control A) spent about 82% of generations inside the Goldilocks band because it barely moved. The unified stack that was forced to transform, restructure, and contain every generation scored only ~23%. Evolution efficiency E/(D+ε) already showed the asymmetry: Control A ~0.044 · Control B ~0.054 · Unified ~0.028. Ablating open-loop φ nearly recovered ordinary Goldilocks rates — the proportional stage was overdriving, not “φ is false as an idea.”

EPH-RH-D (locked). Equalizing path length and treating φ as a regulator flipped the story versus Control A: regulator+prime posted higher useful evolution and higher E/(D+ε) (~0.029 vs ~0.011) with slightly lower bleed. Matched non-prime containment with the same regulator was still more efficient (~0.034) than prime (~0.029). Random novelty led the board. Homeostatic signature rate was 0.

EPH-RH-D2 (locked). Exact transformation budget Q_max = 2.4 and loss-aware fidelity F = (E·R·I)/(L+λB+ε) made the dynamical board fair. Coupled adaptive + prime beat Control A and matched non-prime on F — but sequential pipeline still beat coupled interaction, open-loop φ still beat adaptive φ, random still led F, and new-attractor homeostasis stayed at rate 0.

EPH-IA (locked withhold) — information ledger. Prior layers tested constants as transformation operators when the deeper hypothesis is that they organize information. EPH-IA runs an encode → store → transform → retrieve → reconstruct ledger with matched representation budgets (84 slots). Live IA board: φ hierarchy alone leads IFE (~1.381). Every arm that includes the e-update kernel loses recursive conservation (RCR falls from ~0.72 to ~0.61). Full stack IFE ~1.15 — below A, below best single (φ), below best pair (φ+prime). Complementarity fails. Gate false. Locked.

EPH-IA-R (live engine gate) — e reversibility + dual-state verify/commit. The striking IA pattern was not “the combination failed.” It was that every architecture containing the present e-update collapses into the same lower RCR regime. That makes e the first thing to investigate. IAR freezes the IA board and runs a targeted set: e-forward only; e-forward plus exact inverse; e-forward plus lossless delta with identity retained; e on an explore copy while identity stays untouched; and a dual-state pipeline Transform → Organize → Contain → Verify → Commit, where explore may mutate but identity commits only when recoverability, bleed, and useful evolution clear pre-registered thresholds.

Live IAR board: e-forward RCR ~0.611 versus Control A ~0.720 — the destroyer pattern replicates. Exact inverse restores RCR (~0.721). Lossless identity-retain leads RCR (~0.750) and useful-change efficiency E/(L+ε) (~0.082). Dual-state verify→commit beats e-forward on RCR (~0.711) with commit rate ~0.24 and preserves identity, but does not beat Control A on RCR and loses on E/(L+ε) because rejected explores count as zero committed evolution — the gate is currently too stingy for useful change. IAR gate: false. Engine shelf: WITHHOLD. The combination is not falsified; the present e coupling is.

The problem after “nothing to crown”

Recursive pipelines — summarizers that rewrite their own summaries, policies that revise policies, household tools that “helpfully” reorganize last month’s categories — fail in two everyday ways. They freeze: the rent line never moves, the job description never absorbs a new duty, the model never admits the world changed. Or they dissolve: each rewrite is locally plausible, yet after twenty passes the document no longer matches what legal approved, what payroll can pay, or what a parent can explain to a child. Engineers often measure one-step error and miss the slope. ERFT measured cumulative drift under matched recursion and refused a magic-number story. That refusal does not end the design problem. It relocates it.

The dynamical forks asked whether e, φ, and primes could steer a changing state without dissolving it. Those boards taught useful engineering lessons — Goldilocks hit-rate can reward stagnation; origin distance is the wrong denominator for legitimate change; exact budgets matter; open-loop φ can overdrive — but they never measured storage efficiency, retrieval precision, or reconstruction fidelity. If the real claim is that prime factorization helps addressing, that φ helps hierarchical allocation, and that e helps continuous update without full re-encode, then the battlefield has to be an information ledger. That is the fork EPH-IA takes.

The unified experiment and the mechanism test

The architecture under test began as one sequential dynamical system: each generation applies an e-like continuous transform, then a φ-like proportional structuring, then a prime-defined containment step. Senior audit of that board, and of the matched-budget and loss-aware forks that followed, found a category error: the experiment was testing controllers, not information architecture. EPH-IA does not rewrite those nulls. It adds a mechanism test beside them.

On the ledger, every arm receives the same slot budget. Encode builds a hierarchical block pyramid. Store keeps the slots. Transform applies either a plain mild update or an e-derived continuous EMA. Retrieve pulls by address. Reconstruct fills coverage from stored blocks. Ablations strip components one at a time and in pairs so any advantage can be attributed. The experiment can fail cleanly — and on the live board it does.

Hypothesis and falsifiers

The information-architecture hypothesis said e, φ, and prime structure may be complementary organization primitives. The live IA board falsified that package on these fixtures: φ hierarchy is a mild IFE leader; the e-update kernel costs conservation; primes do not uniquely win IFE; the product is not complementary. IAR then asked a cleaner question: does e create useful change when its information-loss channel is removed, and can a dual-state system evolve an explore copy without evolving away the identity required to reconstruct the source? The answer on these fixtures is mixed and honest. Removing the loss channel restores conservation. Dual-state commit beats destructive e-forward on recoverability. It still fails to beat ordinary recursion on RCR and on useful evolution per unit loss, because the verify gate currently rejects most explores. That is a systems-architecture finding — transform ≠ storage ≠ identity — not a quiet rescue of φ or a claim that primes never help.

Why the framing still helps even when results are mixed

Methodologically, this expedition keeps scientific questions from being smuggled into each other. ERFT asked whether a numerical constant alone moves cumulative drift. A dynamic lane asked whether switching among constants beats the best fixed policy. The homeostasis layers asked whether a compositional controller can produce bounded evolution. EPH-IA asks whether the same mathematical structures improve the information lifecycle itself. Separating the questions is the point. It prevents a favorite constant from being smuggled back in as “the combination secretly meant φ all along,” and it prevents a pretty story about primes from being mistaken for a database index after you only ever tested a firewall.

That posture matters outside the lab. People who ship recursive tools for jobs, rent, and kids need a vocabulary that can say “we changed on purpose” without saying “we leaked identity,” and a second vocabulary that can say “we stored and retrieved the rent line correctly” without confusing that claim with “our controller stayed pretty.” Distinguishing dynamics from representation is how you refuse to congratulate a system for wandering farther from payroll truth — or for filing the truth under the wrong address.

What comes next without rewriting the baseline

ERFT-V5, ERFT-D, V1 Goldilocks null, EPH-RH-D matched-budget null, EPH-RH-D2 loss-aware withhold, and EPH-IA information-architecture withhold stay locked. This expedition does not reopen them as “φ won after all,” and it does not silently retune e to erase the RCR collapse. Engine pin stays off. The honest next step, if any, is confirmatory work on held-out fixtures with dual-state thresholds frozen before looking — or a clear stop if gated commit repeatedly fails to produce useful evolution without dissolving identity.
