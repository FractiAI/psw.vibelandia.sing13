When GPU counters lie, treat them like phenotypes

SS Vibelandia — 2026-06-17 · Fair Exchange

You can feel the fork without a PhD. Grocery bills and rent still bite. News keeps shouting about smarter machines and bigger maps. At the same time, people you know are hunting for work, holding two gigs, or watching a side hustle go quiet. Here is the same idea in kitchen-table English: Modern GPU profiling still often pretends hardware counters are a fixed generative truth. Compilers rewrite kernels. Schedulers shuffle streams. CUPTI sampling injects observation noise. On an H100-class evaluation desk, a counter spike can mean a real bottleneck — or a phenotype that only appeared because the observer looked. The Epigenetic Execution-State Model — EESM — reframes those traces as conditionally expressed phenotypes of a latent execution genome, so teams can talk about invariance instead of chasing ghosts.

Counters as phenotypes, not scripture

The structural causal model layers the story: a latent execution genome; epigenetic regulators such as compiler fusion; observational modulation from sampling; a phenotype of raw counters; a bounded embedding into instruction pressure, memory traffic, and stall geometry; and a decoded regime label for bottleneck phase. Mechanism invariance means observation and decode maps stay fixed under bounded interventions.

Epigenetic here is a computational and causal-modeling metaphor. Say that sentence early in serious rooms. Hardware does not grow a genome. Clinics stay clinics. The metaphor exists so engineers can name observer effects without calling each other irrational.

Why production teams feel the pain

Triton, TorchInductor, and CUTLASS stacks invalidate naive observational assumptions weekly. If your autoscaler treats every counter shift as new ground truth, you will optimize noise. EESM’s claim is narrower: regime stability can be preserved when the observation manifold remains injective over a restricted identifiable support — and collapses when geometry degenerates.

That narrower claim is still huge for teams who live in telemetry. It gives language for when a dashboard lies. It budgets for sampling as part of the system, not as an afterthought apology.
