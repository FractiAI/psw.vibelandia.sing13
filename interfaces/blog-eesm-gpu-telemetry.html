When GPU counters lie, treat them like phenotypes

SS Vibelandia — 2026-06-17 · Fair Exchange

Modern GPU profiling still often pretends hardware counters are a fixed generative truth. Compilers rewrite kernels. Schedulers shuffle streams. CUPTI sampling injects observation noise. The Epigenetic Execution-State Model — EESM — reframes those traces as conditionally expressed phenotypes of a latent execution genome, so teams can talk about invariance instead of chasing ghosts.

Anyone who has watched a “regression” vanish after a Triton upgrade knows the feeling. The counters moved. The story told by the counters may not have been about the workload you thought you measured. Autoscalers that treat every counter shift as new ground truth will optimize noise with great confidence. EESM’s gift is vocabulary: latent genome, epigenetic regulators, observational modulation, phenotype, embedding, decoded regime — a structural causal model for execution analysis, not a claim that silicon has DNA.

The structural causal stack

The model layers the story carefully. A latent execution genome stands for the program’s intended computational identity. Epigenetic regulators — compiler fusion, scheduling choices, runtime flags — shape how that identity is expressed. Observational modulation from sampling and counter windows adds another filter. The phenotype is the raw counter vector you actually see. A bounded embedding projects that phenotype into instruction pressure, memory traffic, and stall geometry. A decoded regime label names the bottleneck phase.

Mechanism invariance, in this framing, means observation and decode maps stay fixed under bounded interventions. When the observation manifold remains injective over a restricted identifiable support, regime stability can be preserved. When geometry degenerates, invariance collapses — and the paper wants you to notice the collapse instead of inventing a new myth about “the GPU changed personality.”

Why production stacks need this language

Triton, TorchInductor, and CUTLASS stacks invalidate naive observational assumptions weekly. MLPerf and CUTLASS reference material plus microarchitectural perturbations typical of H100-class evaluation talk supply the corpus lane for the paper’s telemetry claims. The claim is narrower than a procurement certificate: regime stability is discussable when the causal story is explicit; it is not discussable when teams treat counters as oracles.

In guest English: your counters are a look, not the soul. Compilers and samplers change the look. If your autoscaler chases every look, you will tune noise. EESM asks you to name the latent work, name the regulators, and ask whether the regime label still means the same thing after an intervention.

Epigenetic is a metaphor — keep the coat labeled

The word “epigenetic” travels dangerously well into biology headlines. The honesty rail is blunt: this is a computational and causal-modeling metaphor for GPU execution analysis — not biology, not clinical advice, and not a claim that hardware has a genome. Results are research telemetry claims, not procurement certificates and not medical analogies with teeth.

Interventions, injectivity, and autoscaler hygiene

Bounded interventions are the practical heart of EESM. Change a compiler flag, fuse a kernel, alter CUPTI windows, reshuffle streams — then ask whether the decode map still names the same bottleneck regime. If the observation manifold stays injective on the support you care about, regime labels can remain meaningful. If geometry collapses, stop optimizing and start diagnosing the map.

Autoscaler hygiene follows. Treat phenotype drift as a hypothesis generator, not as an automatic scale-up trigger. Require a short causal story before spending money: which regulator moved, which observation window changed, which regime label shifted, and whether the shift survives a second look under a fixed decode. MLPerf and CUTLASS corpora give shared language for those looks; they are not procurement medals by themselves.
