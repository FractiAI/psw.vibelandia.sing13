When GPU counters lie, treat them like phenotypes

SS Vibelandia — 2026-06-17 · Fair Exchange

Modern GPU profiling still often pretends hardware counters are a fixed generative truth. Compilers rewrite kernels. Schedulers shuffle streams. CUPTI sampling injects observation noise. On an H100-class evaluation desk, a counter spike can mean a real bottleneck — or a phenotype that only appeared because the observer looked. The Epigenetic Execution-State Model — EESM — reframes those traces as conditionally expressed phenotypes of a latent execution genome, so teams can talk about invariance instead of chasing ghosts.

Counters as phenotypes, not scripture

The structural causal model layers the story: a latent execution genome; epigenetic regulators such as compiler fusion; observational modulation from sampling; a phenotype of raw counters; a bounded embedding into instruction pressure, memory traffic, and stall geometry; and a decoded regime label for bottleneck phase. Mechanism invariance means observation and decode maps stay fixed under bounded interventions.

Epigenetic here is a computational and causal-modeling metaphor — not biology, not clinical advice, and not a claim that hardware has a genome. Say that sentence early in serious rooms.

Why production teams feel the pain

Triton, TorchInductor, and CUTLASS stacks invalidate naive observational assumptions weekly. If your autoscaler treats every counter shift as new ground truth, you will optimize noise. EESM’s claim is narrower: regime stability can be preserved when the observation manifold remains injective over a restricted identifiable support — and collapses when geometry degenerates.

That narrower claim is still huge for teams who live in telemetry. It gives language for when a dashboard lies without calling engineers irrational.

Scenes from a lying dashboard

A kernel fusion lands. Counters jump. An on-call engineer almost ships a capacity change. EESM Soft Story asks whether the phenotype changed under an epigenetic regulator while the latent regime stayed put. Sometimes the answer is yes. Sometimes the regime truly moved. The model’s job is to make that question askable.

Another scene: CUPTI sampling noise creates a ghost stall. Teams without observation-modulation language chase the ghost for a week. Teams with EESM language budget for observer effects.
