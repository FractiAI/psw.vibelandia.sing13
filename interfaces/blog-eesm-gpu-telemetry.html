When GPU counters lie, treat them like phenotypes

SS Vibelandia — 2026-06-17 · Fair Exchange

Modern GPU profiling still often pretends hardware counters are a fixed generative truth. Compilers rewrite kernels. Schedulers shuffle streams. CUPTI sampling injects observation noise. The Epigenetic Execution-State Model — EESM — reframes those traces as conditionally expressed phenotypes of a latent execution genome, so teams can talk about invariance instead of chasing ghosts.

The structural causal model layers the story: a latent execution genome; epigenetic regulators such as compiler fusion; observational modulation from sampling; a phenotype of raw counters; a bounded embedding into instruction pressure, memory traffic, and stall geometry; and a decoded regime label for bottleneck phase. Mechanism invariance means observation and decode maps stay fixed under bounded interventions.

Why this matters in production is blunt. Triton, TorchInductor, and CUTLASS stacks invalidate naive observational assumptions weekly. If your autoscaler treats every counter shift as new ground truth, you will optimize noise. EESM’s claim is narrower: regime stability can be preserved when the observation manifold remains injective over a restricted identifiable support — and collapses when geometry degenerates.

The corpus draws on MLPerf / CUTLASS reference material and microarchitectural perturbations typical of H100-class evaluation talk. Open the whitepaper for Document ID, honesty boundary, and the full invariance ledger. The “epigenetic” metaphor is computational, not clinical.

Honesty boundary: “Epigenetic” here is a computational / causal-modeling metaphor for GPU execution analysis — not biology, not clinical advice, and not a claim that hardware has a genome. Results are research telemetry claims, not procurement certificates.
