GPU Telemetry as Execution Weather

SS Vibelandia — June 2026 · Reno / Truckee corridor

Production teams learn the hard way: a green dashboard can still hide a sick run. Counters lie, or at least they speak a dialect shaped by compilers, schedulers, and runtime weather. EESM treats observable GPU performance traces as conditionally expressed phenotypes of a latent execution genome — a computational metaphor, not biology, not clinical advice, and not hardware-as-genome cosplay. This note is the kitchen-table version for people who have lost sleep to a lying chart.

Everyday stakes are jobs and trust. When telemetry lies, on-call people burn out, launches slip, and the household that depends on that paycheck feels it. Executives who trust a single counter the way some people trust a single headline make the same mistake families make with rumor: one bright number is not the whole story.

Counters as phenotypes, not scripture

A phenotype is what you can see under conditions. Change the compiler flags, the batch shape, the neighbor noise on the machine, and the “same” job can look like a different creature. Classical fixed-generative stories about hardware fail when the environment writes half the script. EESM’s framing keeps that humility online so teams stop worshipping a counter and start reading a corpus of traces with causal care.

Why production teams feel the pain

Scenes from a lying dashboard: utilization looks healthy while tail latency ruins guests; power looks fine while thermal throttling steals a night; a “win” on one GPU generation evaporates after a driver update. People who earn a living keeping clusters honest already know the folklore. This paper files folklore as research telemetry claims with receipts — not procurement certificates, not a promise that your next vendor meeting is settled.
