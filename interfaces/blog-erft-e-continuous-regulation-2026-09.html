After Nothing to Crown, Does Continuous Regulation Clear the Bar?

FractiAI Research — September 28, 2026

When a careful experiment refuses a favorite answer, the next temptation is to invent a more elaborate controller and call the refusal temporary. The EGS Recursive Fidelity Test had already said, under constant-neutral recursion, that φ did not win a clean numerical crown, and that switching among a handful of fixed constants did not beat the best fixed policy on its fixtures. The honest follow-on is not a rescue narrative. It is a new held-out board, a continuous version of the same idea, and a gate written down before looking — including the rule that this work enters the Infinite Octaves engine shelf only if that gate passes.

What the live run found

Observed answer — nothing to crown for the engine. On the confirmatory held-out fixture family, continuous regulation and discrete switching both failed to beat best fixed by the pre-registered fifteen-percent margin. The ENGINE_SHELF inclusion gate failed. This paper ships as an application companion only. It does not rewrite Version Five or the discrete dynamic extension, and it does not pin into the Infinite Octaves engine on the strength of curiosity alone.

If you want the constant named: among the four fixed named arms on held-out, mean final drift edged toward e ≈ 2.718 (about 1.106 NRMSE), ahead of φ (about 1.114), √2 (about 1.115), and baseline (about 1.119). That is a fixture-local ordering, not a recommendation to rebuild every recursive pipeline around e. The stronger control — picking the best of those four separately in each matched cell — sat near 1.088 mean NRMSE.

If you want the combination named: there was not a winning combination. The best dynamic policy on held-out was an adaptive discrete look-ahead (about 1.096) — still worse than that per-cell best fixed. Continuous gradient look-ahead landed near 1.098; continuous proportional nudging near 1.120; a random-walk continuous control near 1.121. Improvement versus best fixed was about −0.8%. So the live answer to “is continuous switching the missing piece?” is no, not on these fixtures, and not enough to clear the gate.

The problem the follow-on inherits

Recursive systems fail by degrees. A summarizer softens numbers each pass. A household budget “helpfully” reorganizes until the rent line no longer matches the lease. Engineers often measure one-step error and miss the slope across generations. ERFT measures that slope as normalized distance from the original series and asks whether nesting constants — fixed, sequenced, or state-dependent — change the cumulative curve under matched recursion.

Version Five separated numerical φ from φ-shaped algebra and returned a mixed/null causal readout for the number alone. The discrete dynamic extension asked whether switching among baseline, √2, φ, and e could beat the best fixed constant; on its board, it could not. Those findings stay locked. The follow-on expedition does not get to reopen them by building a smoother controller and pretending the earlier nulls were misunderstandings.

What ERFT-E changes

Two additions, both falsifiable. First, a confirmatory held-out family frozen before looking: chirp, step-recovery, heavy-tail, dual-season, and mean-reverting-jump series under a new seed, not the development fixtures used to tune intuition. Second, continuous regulation: the nest constant may move smoothly inside [1, 2.5] instead of jumping among a discrete set — proportional nudges from measured drift change, and a one-step gradient look-ahead on a fine grid. Discrete state and adaptive policies remain as peers. A random-walk continuous controller sits as a complexity baseline so a busy dial is not mistaken for intelligence.

Before any numbers were read, the team registered an engine-inclusion gate. To recommend an Infinite Octaves ENGINE_SHELF pin, held-out dynamic performance had to beat best fixed by at least fifteen percent, agree in direction with the train family, clear a complexity-adjusted score, show a paired bootstrap interval that excludes zero in favor of the dynamic policy, and do so without a φ-only grammar affordance. Fail any clause and the work ships as an application companion only. The shelf is earned by clearing that gate, not assumed because a follow-on paper exists.

How the scoreboard stays honest

Every arm uses the same constant-neutral recursion topology Version Five locked for causal comparison — unit-normalized nest weights, generation-matched lags and blocks, frozen evaluator, no private φ closure. Drift is normalized RMSE so scale differences across series kinds do not dominate. For each matched cell the suite stores best fixed among named arms, each dynamic policy’s final drift, switching cost, and parameter path length, then summarizes means and a bootstrap interval on the paired gap.

On the live confirmatory board, that honesty is the finding. Adaptive discrete came closest among controllers and still lost to per-cell best fixed. Continuous gradient, despite dozens of candidate probes per step, did not purchase a significant edge. Random walk performed about as poorly as naive proportional nudging. Train-family hints did not survive as a significant held-out win. The gate’s four empirical clauses all returned false; only the architectural clause — no φ-only affordance — passed, which is how the harness was built, not a prize.

Why a careful negative still helps

Refusing an engine pin is itself a result. Guests and evaluators should be able to see that FractiAI will run the next obvious controller, publish the numbers, and decline to promote the work into the Infinite Octaves shelf when the pre-registered bar is missed. That keeps Version Five’s mixed/null numerical readout and the discrete dynamic weakening from being quietly overwritten by a smoother story. It also keeps “significant improvement” from meaning “we wrote another paper.”

Methodologically, the expedition separates three questions that rhetoric often conflates: whether a new fixture family flips the discrete dynamic story, whether continuous $c_n$ buys what discrete modes did not, and whether either result is large and accountable enough for an engine pin. The live answers are no, no, and no — on these fixtures, under this gate. A null that was pre-authorized is more useful than a soft win that was not.

What comes next without rewriting the baseline

ERFT-V5 and ERFT-D remain the locked confirmatory and exploratory baselines. ERFT-E remains the confirmatory-held-out and continuous-regulation expedition that failed its own engine gate. Next honest moves, if any, are new independent domains with the same gates frozen before looking — not a return to φ-privileged grammar, and not a shelf pin by editorial override. If a future controller clears the fifteen-percent margin, the complexity score, and the paired interval on held-out data, then — and only then — the engine shelf conversation reopens.

If you build or audit recursive systems, the invitation is practical. Read the protocol, inspect the gate clauses, and rerun the pipeline from the standalone repository FractiAI/synthobs-erft-e-continuous-regulation. Parent ERFT remains at FractiAI/synthobs-egs-recursive-fidelity-test. Change the held-out family or the continuous policy, hold the evaluator still, and see whether your domain clears the bar this run did not. That is the point: not a slogan about golden ratios or continuous control, but a reproducible place to argue about cumulative drift with numbers — and a documented refusal when the numbers do not earn the shelf.

Honesty rail: ERFT-E is a controlled catalog expedition—confirmatory held-out plus continuous regulation—not a claim that e, φ, or continuous controllers are physical constants or universal optima. Suite pass means protocol integrity. ENGINE_SHELF inclusion requires the pre-registered significant-improvement gate; the live run failed that gate, so this work is an application companion only and does not rewrite ERFT-V5 or ERFT-D. Fifteen percent is an engineering reporting band, not a validated effect size. Document ERFT-E-2026-09-28.
