The Drift Test Had a Cheat Code. We Removed It.

SS Vibelandia — September 25, 2026 · Reno / Truckee corridor

Every operator who has watched an AI stack rewrite last quarter’s playbook knows the quiet tax: the tools still look busy, the dashboards still glow green, and somehow the story that used to match the kitchen table no longer matches the board packet. Call that drift. It is how paychecks get mis-aimed, how product promises go soft, and how families lose trust in systems that were supposed to help them keep jobs and rent covered.

This ship already published a controlled answer — the EGS Recursive Fidelity Test, ERFT for short. Five arms. Matched recursion. One dial changes. The dinner-table question stays the same: when the same information is transformed again and again, does baking in the golden nesting ratio near 1.618 slow how fast meaning drifts, compared with the identical process without that constant, and compared with other famous constants in its place?

Version One of that harness had a bug wearing a lab coat. Baseline — the “no special constant” arm at one — kept posting the lowest drift on the mechanisms that matter most. Player 1 said the quiet part out loud: baseline should not produce the least drift. That is not a brand preference. That is a fair-fight rule. So we opened the notebook, found the cheat, and shipped Version Two.

Why a “winning” baseline can still be a bad test

Self-rewriting tools summarize the summary of the summary. They compress the strategy memo into the slide deck, then compress the deck into the agent prompt, then compress the prompt into next week’s “improved” workflow. After enough loops, the original customer promise, the original safety line, or the original hours a parent promised a kid after school has wandered out of the room. Laid-off workers meet the same pattern on the job board: listings rewritten until the human story no longer matches the opening. Drift is the rent you pay when recursion outruns fidelity.

Companies that measure that rent can still automate. Companies that measure it with a crooked ruler ship autonomy theater with better charts. Version One’s ruler was crooked in a specific way: on hierarchical resolution and recursive compression, the block size itself grew with the constant. A constant of one got the finest mesh — the mildest mash. Scaling and recursive weighting were nearly identity transforms, so every arm “tied” at zero while the coarsening dials quietly crowned baseline. Feedback used gain one-over-c, so larger constants looked calmer by construction. The scoreboard was not lying about the math it ran. It was lying about the question we thought we asked.

That is the executive nightmare in miniature. You freeze the evaluator. You match the recursion. You still let the control arm inherit the softest job. Then you publish a null that sounds humble while the harness never gave the favorite — or the decoys — a fair chance to fail for the right reasons.

How Version Two makes the fight fair

ERFT is still not “our model versus your model.” Data, depth, evaluator, and compute habit stay frozen. The only intentional difference is still the constant threaded into the recursion. Five arms still run side by side: baseline at one, the golden ratio near 1.618, square root of two, e, and seeded random draws from a fixed interval.

What changed is the meaning of that dial. Transform severity is now fixed across arms. The constant picks geometry — which chord to shear along, which pair of fixed lags to blend, which phase to reinject after a shared compression bite — not how hard the machine mashes the series. Hierarchical and compression blocks no longer grow with the constant. Feedback no longer hides behind inverse gain. Scaling and weighting no longer sit at identity. A new lock, E0b, fails the suite if those cheat codes return: identity traps, monotone coarsening, inverse-gain feedback, or baseline owning a majority of mechanism classes by construction.

That is the same discipline a payroll team already understands. If two contractors bid the same scope, you do not hand the control bidder a shorter punch list and then congratulate yourself when they “win” on cost. You keep the scope matched. You change one variable. You read the invoice.

What Version Two’s scoreboard actually shows

Nine locked experiments passed as protocol — including the anti-bias lock. Protocol pass still does not mean the golden ratio owns the pier. It means the arms ran, the metrics logged, and baseline stopped inheriting least drift from a softer mash.

On the five-arm matched runs, named-arm winners flipped the old story. Scaling leaned golden on five of six domains. Recursive weighting leaned golden on four. Recursive feedback went golden on all six domains we scored for that class. Hierarchical resolution and recursive compression went mixed across square-root-of-two, e, and the golden arm — not baseline-dominated. Mean final drift across the matched cells put the golden arm lowest among the crowded field; baseline sat among the highest. That is the opposite of Version One’s construction gift.

The blind constant ladder under recursive compression still refuses a coronation. Lowest drift landed near two. The golden ratio ranked sixth. Baseline at one ranked last — highest drift on that ladder. The continuous sweep with a held-out split put both train and hold minima near 1.75, not near 1.618. Drift-slope snapshots under the shared fixed-block compression bite plateau near the same final error for every named arm: severity parity holding, no free attractor handed out on that single fixture.

So the honest executive sentence is longer than a headline and shorter than a sermon. We repaired the cheat. Baseline is no longer the least-drift champion by construction. The golden arm leads several matched mechanism classes and posts the best average on that board. The pre-registered strong claim — advantage across domains, decoys, blind ladder, and held-out sweep together — is still not fully cashed. Partial leads are interesting. They are not a crown. Publish both.

What the repair is worth on Main Street

Wall Street loves a story where the proprietary constant always wins. Operators who still have to make payroll should prefer a different story: when the harness is wrong, you fix the harness in public; when the favorite only partly wins, you say partly; when the held-out sweep points somewhere else, you leave that door open for the next kid who will inherit these tools.

A repaired null-or-partial also protects the brand more than a flattering chart ever could. Parents trying to keep a school night coherent, freelancers trying to keep billable notes true enough to cover rent, and teams trying to keep a customer promise from dissolving under agent rewrites all need the same habit: freeze the evaluator, match the recursion, change one dial, and refuse to let the control arm inherit the softest job.

For executives, the management takeaway is sharper than any ratio. If your preferred nesting habit only “wins” because the baseline mash was milder, you do not have a nesting insight — you have a softer punch list. Version Two is how this ship keeps curiosity doors open without selling colder doors dressed as destiny.

This is an application companion sitting beside the voyage — a test you can run, not a crown for the golden nesting key. Later versions can attach live public downloads and tighter metrics without rewriting the honesty rule. If a future fixture set shows a persistent golden advantage across domains, decoys, ladder, and hold-out together, we will publish that too. Until then, the pier line stays executive-clear: we found the cheat, we removed it, baseline stopped winning least drift by construction, and the strong claim still has homework.

Closing pier

Watch paychecks and product promises, not just model demos. When tools rewrite themselves, drift is the quiet tax on trust. Version Two of this recursive fidelity test puts that tax on a scoreboard five arms wide — without handing the control arm a softer mash. The suite passed. The cheat is gone. The golden ratio near 1.618 leads some fair classes and still does not cash every pre-registered bar. That honesty is how a ship keeps Main Street in the conversation while the Intelligence Age keeps climbing. Fix the dial. Publish the board. Keep people able to earn, ask, and belong.
