The Drift Test That Finally Measures Nesting

When your team’s AI stack rewrites its own playbooks every week, the bill shows up as drift: job descriptions that no longer match the work, customer promises that soften between sprints, kids asking why the family budget spreadsheet “fixed itself” and got worse. The EGS Recursive Fidelity Test is the ship’s attempt to score that tax in public — same data, same depth, one dial changed. Version Three is live because the last dial was measuring the wrong physics.

What broke in the old scoreboard

Version One handed baseline the softest mash. Version Two fixed that fairness problem but still threaded the constant through a sine wave — pretty geometry, not the nest-ratio grammar the catalog uses when it talks about recursive self-improvement. You were watching a fair fight on the wrong field. Operators felt it: the golden ratio was supposed to be the nesting key near 1.618, yet the blind ladder still buried it while decoys floated ahead. That mismatch was a test-design bug, not proof that nature prefers mediocrity.

Version Three does what a senior test engineer should do first: align the fixture with the claim. Each arm still shares data, evaluator, and recursion depth. Only the nest ratio changes. The golden arm uses the exact self-similar split — weights 1/φ and 1/φ² that sum to one and satisfy whole-to-part equals part-to-remainder. Baseline stays explicit dyadic one-to-one, not a degenerate one-over-c trick. Decoys use normalized partitions at √2 and e. Transform severity stays fixed so nobody inherits a softer punch list.

What the board says now

Ten locked experiments pass, including a new grammar lock that fails the suite if φ’s partition is not self-similar. On the five-arm matched runs across synthetic and proxy domains, mean final drift for the golden arm lands near 10.7 versus 13.8 for baseline — about twenty-three percent lower on the aggregate that matters for payroll-grade decisions. That gap is wide enough to feel in a board packet, not just a lab notebook.

Class-by-class, φ wins recursive feedback and recursive compression outright — the mechanisms that look most like “observe your last output and re-enter the loop.” Scaling on these fixtures still favors dyadic baseline; weighting and hierarchy split among decoys. The blind ladder, now averaged across all mechanisms instead of one cherry-picked path, ranks φ third of seven — not a coronation, but no longer the buried sixth-place story from the old geometry. The continuous sweep’s minima still sit away from 1.618 on held-out series. Honest scoreboard: strong aggregate golden lead, mixed per-class bar, no fake crown.

Version Three is the habit this ship wants: name the protocol, publish the board, fix the harness when the claim and the fixture disagree, and keep null results on file beside wins. Prior receipts stay in the footnotes — Version One’s coarsening bias, Version Two’s sin geometry — so nobody quotes stale numbers as today’s weather.

How the harness actually runs

Picture five contractors asked to remodel the same kitchen with the same inspector and the same deadline. Each crew must use an identical recursive playbook: compress the plan, feed back corrections, re-weight yesterday’s measurements, and climb one more floor of detail. The only difference is the nesting ratio baked into how they split counter space and recall prior cuts. One crew uses plain half-and-half dyadic splits. Another uses the golden partition where the long segment over the short one keeps repeating the same proportion. The others use famous irrationals as decoys so “any weird constant helps” cannot pass unnoticed.

Every generation, the evaluator asks a blunt question: how far did today’s layout drift from the original blueprint? Recursive Fidelity Drift is that distance — root-mean-square first, with mean absolute error and correlation riding along as witnesses. The suite also fits a drift slope across generations so a lucky first pass cannot hide a runaway rewrite. Blind ladders and held-out series splits stay in the protocol so curve-fitting one pretty chart cannot declare victory. When the nest grammar lock fails, the pipeline stops before anyone tweets a crown. That is the difference between architecture theater and a test you can argue with on the pier.

Automated vitest guards now encode the architectural intent: φ’s partition must close self-similarly, baseline must stay dyadic, and the aggregate matched runs must show φ with the lowest mean drift unless a future honest refactor disproves it. Those are guardrails on the instrument, not a promise that nature owes you golden ratios in every domain on earth.
