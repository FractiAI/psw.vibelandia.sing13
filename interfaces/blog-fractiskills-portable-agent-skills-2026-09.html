Your Nested Agents Need a Carry-On, Not a Shipping Container

FractiAI Research — September 11, 2026

When helpers nest, someone has to tell each child which room they are in. Most teams solve that the expensive way: paste the whole corpus into every prompt, watch the token meter climb, then wonder why the humans cleaning up the fog lost hours — then overtime, then a quieter job board. FractiSkills files a different habit. Portable, page-scoped skill packs — short SKILL.md packages — sit under the AI catalog layer and load pointer-first into Lattice Chat. The question is operational and falsifiable in spirit: do carry-on packs reduce contradictory handoffs and cleanup loops compared with shipping-container prompts, for people who still have rent due?

The problem nested agents create without a shared room

Daniel Ari Friedman’s original FractiSkills work named the pattern — one portable agent skill per page. SING 13’s filing is the local Lattice Chat companion: a monorepo corpus on the order of hundreds of skills (about 421 in the local index), generated from registries, consumed with tight budgets. Guests meet hospitality. Builders meet receipts. Families meet the cost of fog when dialect islands confuse real people at work and at school.

Portable means a skill pack can move with the surface — which paper, which ship-blog note, which protocol, which major door — without embedding a foreign marketplace corpus as production proof. Frontmatter names the room. A short body orients the child. An index keeps the library findable. Skills complement whitepapers; they do not replace them. Parents teaching kids already know the difference: a one-page study guide for Tuesday’s test beats dumping the entire classroom library into a backpack. Nested helpers are not children, but the rhyme holds. When every child invents a private handbook, guests get contradictory answers, support tickets multiply, and the humans who were supposed to ship spend their paycheck week untangling fog instead of craft.

What pointer-first loading measured in practice

The hypothesis is operational. If nested children load a handful of matched pointers and at most a couple of pinched bodies per turn — metabolize the ask, crystallize a short pointer pack, animate with local texture, then scale-to-zero when drift is done — token burn and dialect islands should fall relative to paste-everything habits. Nested children do not mesh-sync the full corpus. The parent picks. The household parallel is familiar: one shared chore list beats five private calendars fighting in the living room.

A skill pack is a short package plus index: enough to orient a child to a surface, not enough to pretend the child swallowed the library. The local pipeline discovers surfaces from registries — papers, ship-blog, protocols, research suites, major interfaces — renders SKILL.md packages, and writes an index. Curated Lattice and Core meta skills stay in the mix. Regenerating when registries change keeps the corpus resonant with what the project actually publishes. CEOs feel the bill as budget, dialect islands as support tickets, and unclear doors as stalled hires and demos that slip. Portable skills are an operations story first: less paste, more pointer, shared nouns across children.

The qualification matters. Flat summaries that upgrade a carry-on into “infinite GPU-free scaling proved” invite myth. Banded briefs invite re-runs. FractiSkills sits under the AI catalog layer as an application companion consumed by Lattice Chat. It is not an engine pin on the Infinite Octaves sync stack. CMOS, tensor, and master synthesis stay where silicon auditors expect them. About 1.618 supplies nesting grammar for how skill pointers nest under catalog briefs. The companion does not embed Skillarum, does not treat a live 405-page public crawl as production proof, and does not prove infinite GPU-free scaling. Myth is expensive for people who still have rent due on the first of the month.

Why portable skills still help

Methodologically, FractiSkills separates two questions teams often merge. First: can you define page-scoped skill packs precisely enough that nested agents inherit a room instead of a private handbook? Second: under that definition, does pointer-first loading cool invoices and cleanup loops relative to shipping-container prompts? The local companion answers the first with registries and SKILL.md packages and gives a practical answer to the second: fewer contradictory answers, fewer cleanup loops, fewer humans losing hours to fog — hours that are paychecks, and paychecks that are rent and groceries.

That mixed, operational read is what a senior engineering lead should want. It prevents vanity heat that loads every skill “just in case,” and it prevents marketing fog from stealing a Tuesday design review by confusing application companions with engine pins. Tuesday work for operators is boring on purpose: regenerate when registries change, prefer pointer-first prompts, audit a sample of packs so honesty stays at the end rail of public notes, and answer spend questions with doors and tickets rather than mysticism.

If you nest agents, the invitation is concrete. Open Lattice Chat. Pick a surface you care about — a paper, a ship-blog note, a protocol. Watch whether the helper still knows the room after a handoff. If it invents a private handbook, the skill pack failed the hospitality test. If it points and stays scoped, the corpus is doing its job. Read the whitepaper for the filing cabinet. Credit Daniel’s original paper and repo when you cite the pattern. Keep skills short, scoped, and honest. Keep engine pins separate from application companions. Keep human emergency above every clever map. That is the point: nested helpers can share rooms without pasting the whole town into every prompt, and without forgetting that people still need clear doors to earn a living.
