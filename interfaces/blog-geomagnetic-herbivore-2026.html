Do bison follow storms? An honest collar study

SS Vibelandia — 2026-06-01 · Fair Exchange

Large herbivores navigate forage, water, fencing, and weather. Whether Earth’s magnetic field or geomagnetic storms measurably perturb those movements is an open empirical question — especially for American bison, where open collar archives are sparse. This study runs a conservative, falsification-first answer using public data, and it refuses to upgrade weak correlations into folklore.

Movement comes from publicly downloadable GPS collar fixes on Movebank — not Turner passive synthesis. As of the public catalog scan, no open Bison bison collar study was available; the pipeline selects the best available large-herbivore GPS study (default: Snowy Range moose, Wyoming 2019–2020) and aligns GFZ Kp to that collar observation window. Hypotheses H1–H5 cover heading alignment, storm displacement, corridor gradients, bison-specific claims, and recent anomaly windows.

Primary finding under this collar layer: no strong support for magnetoreceptive navigation (H1) and no_support to weak for storm-associated displacement (H2, H5). Geomagnetic causation is not asserted because weather, management, and habitat confounds are not fully controlled. That negative-leaning result is the useful product.

Keep tiers separate: the companion SynthOBS multi-taxa wavefield paper files structural catalog roles; this empirical note owns the Movebank-plus-Kp tests; Turner Phase 1 remains a pasture-scale fused console. When a public bison Movebank study becomes available, tighten covariates before elevating any hypothesis above moderate support.

Honesty boundary: correlation ≠ causation. Public evidence does not support extraordinary magnetic navigation in bison at this empirical tier. Not space-weather advice, not clinical guidance, and not a claim that herds can be managed by geomagnetic grids alone.
