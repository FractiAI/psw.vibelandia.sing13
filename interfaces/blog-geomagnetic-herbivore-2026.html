Do bison follow storms? An honest collar study

SS Vibelandia — 2026-06-01 · Fair Exchange

Large herbivores navigate forage, water, fencing, and weather. Whether Earth’s magnetic field or geomagnetic storms measurably perturb those movements is an open empirical question — especially for American bison, where open collar archives are sparse. This study runs a conservative, falsification-first answer using public data, and it refuses to upgrade weak correlations into folklore.

Everybody loves a magnetic-animal story. Science loves a refute column. FractiAI’s geomagnetic herbivore note chooses the second love without sneering at the first. The point is not to kill wonder. The point is to keep wonder from hiring itself as a ranch manager.

What data actually walked into the lab

Movement comes from publicly downloadable GPS collar fixes on Movebank — not Turner passive synthesis, not private ranch telemetry cosplay. As of the public catalog scan, no open Bison bison collar study was available. The pipeline therefore selects the best available large-herbivore GPS study (default: Snowy Range moose, Wyoming 2019–2020) and aligns GFZ Kp indices to that collar observation window. That substitution is spoken aloud in the paper because honesty is part of the method.

Hypotheses H1–H5 cover heading alignment, storm displacement, corridor gradients, bison-specific claims, and recent anomaly windows. Primary finding under this collar layer: no strong support for magnetoreceptive navigation (H1) and no_support to weak for storm-associated displacement (H2, H5). Geomagnetic causation is not asserted because weather, management, and habitat confounds are not fully controlled. That negative-leaning result is the useful product.

Why bison-specific claims stay gated

American bison carry cultural weight in Western Soft Story — Turner ranch lore, plains memory, QUESTFEST special-projects curiosity. Cultural weight is not a covariate. Until open Bison bison collar archives exist at usable density, bison-specific magnetoreception claims remain gated behind honesty labels. Moose proxies teach method and limit inference. That is not a slight against bison. It is respect for what the public catalog actually holds.

Managers who want operational guidance should read the confound list twice: weather, fencing, forage, hunting pressure, capture effects, sampling irregularity. Any one of those can mimic a “storm response” in a careless chart. The paper’s refusal to assert geomagnetic causation is the feature guests should quote — not a buried footnote.
