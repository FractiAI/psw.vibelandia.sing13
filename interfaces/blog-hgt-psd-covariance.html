Chromatin maps that stay mathematically legal

SS Vibelandia — 2026-06-17 · Fair Exchange

Predicting 3D chromatin conformation is not only a biology problem — it is a geometry problem. Contact maps that wander outside the positive semi-definite (PSD) cone are mathematically illegal as covariances, no matter how pretty the heatmap looks. Hierarchical Genomic Tokenization with structured PSD covariance operators (HGT-PSD) treats Hi-C prediction as a Conditional PSD Covariance Estimator so predicted contacts stay inside the legal cone by construction.

The model maps genomic sequence to a sequence-dependent basis and defines contact adjacency as Ŷ(x) = Φ(x)Φ(x)ᵀ + diag(σ²(x)) — a Gram matrix plus non-negative diagonal. Proposition 1 is the short proof that every prediction resides in S₊ⁿ. Scaling uses hierarchical tokenization from 250-kb down to 10-kb bins; training stabilizes with an Orthogonal Frobenius-Space Masking Loss across distance-stratified regimes.

Hi-C regression on ENCODE GM12878 matrices is a crowded benchmark field; guarantees that outputs remain PSD are rarer than leaderboard brags. A method that keeps physical constraints in the architecture reduces silent failures when downstream tools assume a valid covariance. UCSC / ENCODE ingest paths keep the corpus public and auditable.
