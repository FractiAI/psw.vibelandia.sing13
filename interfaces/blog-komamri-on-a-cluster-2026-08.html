MRI Across Machines — A Plan, Not a Cluster

SS Vibelandia — August 2026 · Reno / Truckee corridor

One laptop can carry a surprising amount of MRI simulation. One laptop cannot carry an honest story about what happens when the phantoms get heavy and the timelines get long. Synthio’s KomaMRI-on-a-cluster companion is the plan for that next step — how to think about splitting simulation work across machines, who may drive the sandbox seat, and what guests should hear when someone says “cloud MRI.” It is a plan filed beside the MRI cloud-antenna paper — a map for the next room, while the festival edge still treats live multi-machine clusters on serverless functions as future work, not present furniture.

If you only watch demos, you’ll hear “cloud MRI” and imagine a clinical magnet humming in a rack downtown. If you watch the doors on this ship, you’ll see a sandbox: simulation software, catalog metaphors, creator keys, guest hospitality. Racks are not coils. That sentence is the seatbelt. The plan still matters, because soft ceilings are where exploratory fire dies if nobody names the next room — the way a small shop dies when nobody admits the old stove can’t feed the Saturday rush, or the way a family kitchen fails when everyone pretends one oven can bake three trays at once.

Why one machine is a soft ceiling

KomaMRI and related Bloch-timeline tools are serious simulation craft. They let creators explore pulse sequences, phantoms, and timing without a living body in the bore. They also hit memory and wall-clock walls when you scale sequences, resolutions, or ensemble runs. A soft ceiling is not a moral failure. It is an engineering weather report — the same kind of report a kitchen gets when the oven can’t keep up, or a school gets when one classroom can’t hold three grades at once.

Distributed plans answer that weather with shard language: which work splits, which state stays local, which reductions cross the wire, which failures are expected. The whitepaper files that plan under Synthio’s creator-only seat — MRI / cloud-antenna catalog grammar — separate from the Infinite Octaves engine pin. Guests do not get Synthio request handling. Creators do. That privilege split is hospitality, not theater. Families understand doors: some rooms are for hosts, some for visitors, and mixing them without labels is how people get hurt. Neighbors understand the same rhythm when a community workshop keeps certain tools behind a keyed cabinet.
