Research note · LLM Rhyme Expedition

Exploring the LLM Rhyme on Sky Catalogs and the Genome

FractiAI Research — November 18, 2026

This expedition explores the LLM Rhyme: writing public materials as language in three packs — LINEAR (a local window), FLAT (a dump), and AWARE (a ranked relationship board with a leading candidate) — and asking whether a careful reader can still name a hidden long-range partner. The materials are NASA exoplanets, JPL close approaches, OpenNGC deep-sky objects, the WMAP microwave spectrum, and a small public human-genome panel. The language-pack grammar is the question that carries the title. Nested under every layer is the protein-folding rhyme (sequence → board → partner), but the focus here is the LLM Rhyme itself.

What the LLM Rhyme is

Earlier work showed that how you pack a protein, a document, or a macro series as language changes whether a reader recovers a held-out long-range match. LINEAR keeps nearby neighbors. FLAT piles everything without a ranked map. AWARE prints a leading candidate and a ranked board measured from the features. Infinite Octaves ENGINE_SHELF pin #54 is that language-pack rhyme; pin #53 is the protein-folding rhyme nested underneath. Parents waiting on a school seating chart already know the difference between a labeled map and a pile of names that “ought to be enough.”

What “held-out partner” means (follow along here)

Think of a seating chart. We pick one row (the query). From measured relationship numbers we find who it couples with most strongly who is not in the next few seats (the partner). Then we hold that name out: we do not print “the answer is X” in the prompt. We only give the query plus one of the three packs. The test asks: can a careful reader still name that hidden partner?

Query — the row we start from. Partner — the best long-range match from the board. Held out — that match’s id is the answer key; “recovered” means the reader named it. Kitchen example: planet A’s hidden partner is planet Q. LINEAR shows nearby planets, FLAT shows a pile, AWARE shows a ranked list with a leading candidate. Did they name Q?

What we observed

On NASA exoplanets, JPL close approaches, OpenNGC deep-sky objects, and the WMAP microwave spectrum, forty-eight hidden-partner tasks per layer showed the same shape: AWARE packs at full recovery, LINEAR near zero, FLAT near zero. On a public exon panel and on sliding windows of human mitochondrial DNA, the same three packs showed the same shape again — fourteen long-range cross-gene exon tasks and sixty-four mitochondrial windows. When a reader ignored the ranked board and only matched overlapping words, AWARE fell to about zero. So the win is the map in the pack, not lucky word overlap. Analysts who have waited for a spreadsheet join know the difference between a labeled map and a pile of rows.

How novel is that?

Highest novelty here is method: the language-pack grammar that worked on proteins, documents, and macro series still works on public sky files and a public genome panel under a reader that follows the prompt. Moderate novelty is the genome demonstration on small public panels — same packing shape, not a disease finding, not clinical, not wet-lab genetics. Residual physics lives on the Cosmic Rhyme note, which reports two candidates for new physics (WMAP residual lags eleven and fourteen) as a secondary finding under its own residual sieve. This LLM Rhyme note answers a different paycheck question: when sky rows and genome panels are written as language, does the pinned language-pack rhyme still show up? On these fixtures, yes.

Nested map · cosmic data

Read each file as two rhymes. NASA exoplanets: protein-fold nest is planet features as the sequence, cosine couplings as the board, partner planet as the contact; LLM nest is RA-neighbor text as LINEAR, a dump as FLAT, and a leading candidate plus ranked board as AWARE. JPL close approaches: encounter row as sequence, encounter couplings as board, partner encounter as contact; then the same three language packs. OpenNGC: object row, morphology and sky couplings, partner object; then LINEAR / FLAT / AWARE. WMAP spectrum: multipole strip like a macro series, lag-correlation board, partner ell; then the three language packs. Different public files. Same nested grammar.

Nested map · genome demo

The genome arm is a demonstration on public panels, not a clinic. Exon rows: sequence-local neighbors as LINEAR, a dump as FLAT, a ranked exon board as AWARE; the protein-fold nest is exon features → measured board → partner exon. Mitochondrial windows: local sequence windows as LINEAR, a dump as FLAT, a ranked window board as AWARE; the fold nest is window features → board → partner window. Same LLM Rhyme. Different biology theater. No disease claim.
