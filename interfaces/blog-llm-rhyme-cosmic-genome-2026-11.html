When You Pack the Sky and the Genome as Language, What Did We Actually See?

FractiAI Research — November 18, 2026

Short answer up front. We wrote public sky catalogs and a small human-genome panel as language in three styles. The ranked relationship style recovered the held-out partner. The local-window style and the flat dump did not. That is a method observation on materials anyone can re-download. It is not a new force of nature and not a clinic result. And this work is pinned: both the protein-folding rhyme and the LLM language-pack rhyme are FractiSI engine pins on Tiny Footprint / Tiny Gateway — not “unpinned.”

FractiSI engine pin (do not write “no pin”)

Protein-folding rhyme and LLM language-pack rhyme are both pinned to the FractiSI engine. Nested means every sky or genome layer wears both: the fold grammar (parts → board → partner) and the language grammar (LINEAR / FLAT / AWARE packs). Careful claim boundaries still apply. Honesty is not the same as “no pin.” Tiny Gateway remains Infinite Octaves ENGINE_SHELF #52 for memory architecture; this expedition does not invent a new Infinite Octaves shelf number.

What we observed

On NASA exoplanets, JPL close approaches, OpenNGC deep-sky objects, and the WMAP microwave spectrum, forty-eight held-out tasks per layer showed the same shape: AWARE packs at full recovery, LINEAR near zero, FLAT near zero. On a public exon panel and on sliding windows of human mitochondrial DNA, the same three packs showed the same shape again — fourteen long-range cross-gene exon tasks and sixty-four mitochondrial windows. When a reader ignored the ranked board and only matched overlapping words, AWARE fell to about zero. So the win is the map in the pack, not lucky word overlap. Parents who have waited for a school report and analysts who have waited for a spreadsheet join know the difference between a labeled map and a pile of rows that “ought to be enough.”

How novel is that?

Highest novelty here is method: the language-pack grammar that worked on proteins, documents, and macro series still works on public sky files and a public genome panel under a reader that follows the prompt. Moderate novelty is the genome demonstration on small public panels — same packing shape, not a disease finding, not clinical, not wet-lab genetics. Not novel physics: candidate WMAP residual leftovers live on the Cosmic Rhyme note. Not a frontier-model contest: the default receipt needs no API key.

Nested map · cosmic data

Read each file as two rhymes. NASA exoplanets: protein-fold nest is planet features as the sequence, cosine couplings as the board, partner planet as the contact; LLM nest is RA-neighbor text as LINEAR, a dump as FLAT, and a leading candidate plus ranked board as AWARE. JPL close approaches: encounter row as sequence, encounter couplings as board, partner encounter as contact; then the same three language packs. OpenNGC: object row, morphology and sky couplings, partner object; then LINEAR / FLAT / AWARE. WMAP spectrum: multipole strip like a macro series, lag-correlation board, partner ell; then the three language packs. Different public files. Same nested grammar.

Nested map · human genome demo

Exons from GTEx and MyGene with CpG context: exon along the chromosome as sequence, cross-gene exon-feature board, partner exon as contact (same gene treated as trivial); then LINEAR genomic-order text, FLAT dump, AWARE ranked board. Mitochondrial windows from UCSC: DNA window as sequence, GC and CpG and entropy couplings as board, partner window as contact; then the same three language packs. Exploration on public panels — not a slogan that DNA is a chatbot.
