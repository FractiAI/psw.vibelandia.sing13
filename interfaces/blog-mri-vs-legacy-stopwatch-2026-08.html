Architecture arguments die in slogans. Synthio ran a stopwatch instead. Same sandbox work, two styles: redo everything on every connection (legacy mesh), versus share one field and send small phase-acks (MRI-style). The clock is real. The magnet is not.

That distinction is the whole product pitch in miniature. Shared-field thinking is cheaper when the problem allows it. Per-edge recompute is the expensive habit that feels thorough. On the shipped receipt, the shared-field arm was much cheaper than per-edge recompute — hundreds of times on the Node Bloch lane, tens of times on the live KomaMRI lane. Host-dependent. Re-run the suite to refresh the milliseconds. Receipts live next to the paper.

What the stopwatch is — and is not

These are Node Bloch timings plus optional KomaMRI.jl CPU timings on one host. They are not clinical TR/TE. They are not CUDA invoices. They are not proof that Synthio displaces hyperscale data centers, and they are not proof that Synthio is the 99 Octave engine identity. They are a sandbox performance proxy with enough teeth to argue against mesh theater.

For executives evaluating FractiAI’s creator sandbox, the stopwatch answers a narrower question: when we borrow MRI-style shared-field language for compute orchestration, do wall-clock fixtures move? On this host, yes. On your host, re-run. That is the valley’s preferred epistemology — fixtures you can refresh, not press-release multiples frozen forever.
