Research note · Net Zero Expedition 001 · The Missing Half

When You See Only Half the Circle, What Completes the Whole?

FractiAI Research — October 31, 2026

A household budget that lists every paycheck but forgets the line for rent looks complete until the first of the month. A warehouse that counts inbound pallets and outbound trucks without tracking what sits on the floor invents a mystery shortage. The Net Zero Expedition Series opens with a narrower, sharper question: when we observe only part of a structure, what complementary relationships recover the whole—and how do we tell a real completion from a story that merely fits?

The problem of mistaking a half for the inventory

People already practice complementary accounting without naming it. Parents split a school week between two households and still have to make one calendar. Night-shift nurses hand off a patient chart that only works if the day team’s observations are treated as the other half of the same body. Conventional science often presents a finished object—a circle, a spectrum, a force balance—and leaves the complementary bookkeeping implicit. Expedition 001 asks whether making that bookkeeping explicit, first in mathematics and then in runnable code, gives investigators a cleaner place to search for missing pieces without inventing them.

The series refuses to define complementarity as mirror symmetry alone. Equal-and-opposite pairs are one useful case. Unequal, distributed, and nested relationships are also on the map. Net Zero here is a research framework that keeps attention on the whole and on the accounting that makes the whole intelligible—not a slogan that every imbalance automatically hides a secret twin.

What the inaugural lab actually ran

The suite starts with circles and spheres: upper and lower expressions that reconstruct known area and volume. It then applies a general even–odd decomposition under reflection to a messy sampled function—sine, cubic, cosine mixed together—and checks reconstruction to floating-point noise. Three unequal force vectors with magnitudes three, four, and five are summed to a zero resultant. An illustrative material-flow balance recovers a one-kilogram-per-hour accumulation from known input and outputs. A linear constraint is shown to admit many valid hidden pairs. A real random signal’s discrete Fourier transform is checked for conjugate symmetry. Two equal-amplitude waves cancel at one hundred eighty degrees of phase difference in the idealized model.

Under seed twenty twenty-six ten thirty-one, experiments E1 through E8 passed eight of eight. Circle relative error sat near one part in a billion; sphere near one part in ten trillion; function reconstruction near machine epsilon. The force resultant landed exactly at the origin. The material residual closed. Five distinct pairs satisfied the same sum-to-one constraint. Fourier and wave checks cleared their numerical gates. That board is a foundation receipt, not a tour of every forest on Earth.

Hypothesis under test

If complementary structure is more than poetry, then (1) geometric wholes can be rewritten as complementary expressions without losing defining measures, (2) the same idea generalizes beyond circles, (3) balance can arise from unequal parts, (4) missing terms can sometimes be inferred when the system equation is independently specified, and (5) investigators must still face non-uniqueness—many mathematical completions for one observed constraint. The expedition’s engine-shelf seat stays empty on purpose: this launch is an application companion for a new series, with broader cross-domain work still open.

What the mixed board teaches

The most useful negative result may be Experiment F. Knowing that two numbers sum to one does not tell you which pair nature chose. Kids splitting a cookie already know that “fair” and “unique” are different words. An AI that proposes Complementary Structure Inference will need candidate generation, constraint checks, uncertainty reporting, and independent evidence before asserting that a complement has been found. Otherwise the framework becomes an unfalsifiable habit of inventing whatever half makes the ledger close.

Asymmetric forces and material accounting push the same lesson from another angle. A balanced paycheck plan can include unequal line items. A plant balance can hide inventory change. Neither example licenses claiming every social or ecological crisis is merely a missing twin waiting to be named. Rent is still due; the model still has to be right.

Framework value for the trail ahead

Homeostasis Expedition already carries recursive-balance research on its own journey page. Net Zero Expedition now has a parallel trailhead: a guest journey, a whitepaper, a runnable suite, and a standalone repository. The founding directive is short enough to remember on a night shift: find the complement, establish the relationship, account for the whole. Next phases must leave familiar mirror geometry, test irregular shapes, measure real biological or physical systems with declared boundaries, and try blind discovery against held-out data.
