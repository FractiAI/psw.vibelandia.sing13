Omniversal Goldilocks Rideshare Protocol

SS Vibelandia — 2026-07-07 — plain speak · Fair Exchange

Most routing systems optimize for speed and then dump the friction onto streets, drivers, or servers. The Omniversal Goldilocks Rideshare Protocol (OGRP) flips the brief: accept a trip only when density, thermodynamic overhead, generosity, and pacing sit in a Goldilocks band — starting with downtown Reno micro-mobility as the living anchor. That is not a romance about scooters. It is an operational policy with four gates.

Physical couriers, data packets, and attention-conserving humans can share self-similar acceptance rules without pretending one Reno week proves universal optimality. Bloated cloud sprawl is the computational twin of car-centric sprawl. OGRP makes that rhyme operational.

Four gates in guest English

Density anchoring keeps work inside high-density geometric hubs and programmatically disqualifies sprawl configurations. If the trip asks the system to pretend the suburbs are downtown, the gate fails. Thermodynamic minimization prefers near-zero overhead locomotion — walking, cycling, micro-mobility — or local edge compute. If the plan burns heat and miles for theater, the gate fails.

The generosity floor sets value at or above $9 (or equivalent token) to filter predatory extraction. Culture, not decoration: Fair Exchange and the floor travel together. Sovereign pacing de-prioritizes corporate temporal deadlines in favor of energy preservation and localized resonance. Speed is allowed. Speed that erases sovereignty is not the Goldilocks band.

Together the gates refuse a familiar product sin: optimize the metric you can screenshot, externalize the costs you cannot. OGRP files the costs as acceptance criteria. Cities already know that optimizing only for vehicle speed can punish walkers. Cloud platforms already know that optimizing only for request latency can punish energy and attention.
