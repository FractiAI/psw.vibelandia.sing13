Can Relational Rhyme Connect Partners, Questions to Answers, and Seed to Edge Across Infinite Octaves?

FractiAI Research — November 20, 2026

Short answer: Yes. On the regenerable board, FractiSI rhyme recovered partners, question–answer gaps, agent-world complements, and seed-to-edge links at accuracy 1.0, while exact cache stayed at 0 and token-overlap similarity stayed at or below 0.14. Removing rhyme dropped partner recovery from 1.0 to 0.083. The engine-shelf pin stays deferred.

People already live inside unfinished relationships. A job posting and a resume. A support ticket and the missing server state. A family plan written in January and the August calendar that no longer matches it. The words diverge; the underlying link is what still has to be recovered. FractiSI’s Rhyming World Model already showed that shared structure can beat exact caches when wording changes. This expedition asks whether that same operation stretches further — from partner pairs, through questions and answers, through two agents’ private worlds, and finally from a compact seed to a distant edge after many recursive rewrites.

The problem difference creates

Teams fail at correspondence more often than at storage. Two contractors write different notes about the same client and then argue about who is right. A parent asks why the carpool failed and gets a fluent answer that repeats the question’s words while missing the real gap — who held which state when the hand-off happened. A project plan is rewritten eight times until the original purpose is still “in there somewhere,” but nobody can point from today’s edge document back to the seed without a full paste of the old text.

Prior FractiSI work already separated rhyme from copy. Shared-world labs showed that storing a pattern once and writing missions as deltas beats reprinting every ticket. Pilot Rhyme showed that Director and Pilot seats can walk a multi-hop chain when flat peers cannot. What was still open was whether one relational operation covers the progression the product keeps meeting in the field: who belongs with whom, which answer closes which gap, and whether a seed still relates to a far edge after the representation has been rewritten beyond recognition.

What we tested

We locked a definition first. A rhyme exists when differentiated representations relate through a common underlying world structure, so the system can reconstruct, coordinate, complete, or predict across them. That definition refuses shortcuts: textual similarity, embedding proximity, word matching, exact caching, and prefix reuse are baselines, not the claim.

Five levels share one benchmark family. Partner trials ask which entity belongs with which, under paraphrases and distractors that look like the query. Question–answer trials encode an unresolved coordination gap in the world and demand the answer that names the missing relationship, while lexical distractors repeat the question’s words. A multi-agent level splits one relation across two agent worlds. A recursive level starts from a compact seed, rewrites wording, scale, and entity count through eight octaves, and deliberately drops inherited seed text so ordinary propagation cannot cheat. Ablations then remove rhyme, the shared world, the relational index, and selective reconstruction one at a time.

Arms on every level: exact cache, token-Jaccard similarity as a stand-in for embedding closeness, a shared fact bag without a relational index, FractiSI with rhyme removed, and FractiSI rhyme. The seed is fixed so anyone can regenerate the scoreboard.

Hypothesis

If relational rhyme is a general FractiSI operation, then the rhyme arm should recover partners, close relational question gaps, join complementary agent worlds, and hold seed-to-edge links after deep transforms — while exact and lexical arms fail when wording diverges, and removing rhyme should erase the gain. If lexical similarity or shared bags without an index match rhyme, or if seed–edge recovery collapses with octave distance, the unified hypothesis fails.

Results

On 120 partner trials, rhyme accuracy was 1.0 at about 39 bytes mean cost. Exact cache scored 0. Token overlap scored 0.142 — the distractors did their job. The shared bag without a relational index matched that same 0.142 floor. The gap versus the best baseline was +0.858.

On 120 question–answer trials, rhyme scored 1.0 and every lexical and bag baseline scored 0. The lexical trap never won. Rhyme won on every trial where lexical similarity lost. That is the critical fork the protocol demanded: success because the answer rhymes with the world structure of the question, not because it resembles the question’s words.

Agent-world complements told the same story: rhyme 1.0, embedding about 0.14, shared bag 0. Across eight octave depths, rhyme recovery from seed to each octave and to the edge stayed at 1.0 while exact and embedding stayed at 0 when inherited seed text was stripped. The propagation control — copying seed text into the edge — is reported separately so nobody confuses persistence of bytes with relational recovery.

Ablations locate the work. Full rhyme held at 1.0. Minus rhyme fell to 0.083. Minus shared world fell to 0. Minus relational index fell to 0.15. Removing selective reconstruction alone did not drop accuracy on this board, so the differentiator is the index into shared relational structure, not a reconstruction trick. The reported Relational Rhyme Score is 3.678 under the primary formula, with an alternate linear blend at 0.983; both formulations are documented so the metric can be challenged.

Qualifications

Token overlap is a stand-in for embedding proximity, not a neural encoder bake-off. Structural keys are controlled in the lab; a live system still has to induce them. Octave rewrites are representational steps on a controlled lab board rather than a cosmology experiment. Perfect rhyme accuracy is expected when the index is correct and the distractors are surface-based — the result that matters is the gap versus baselines and the collapse under ablation. Previously demonstrated Shared-World and Pilot Rhyme results are cited as prior art, not re-sold as proof of this progression. The engine-shelf pin stays deferred.

Why this earns a Rhyming Expedition seat

The Rhyming World Model needs a clear answer when a customer asks whether “rhyme” is only a compression trick. This arm says the useful meaning is relational continuity across difference. That is the same muscle you want when two agents hold halves of a plan, when a ticket is a question about a missing world link, and when a seed purpose must still be recoverable after months of rewritten edges. For people hiring agents the way they hire people — different seats, shared mission, changing words — the paycheck-level stake is whether the system can find what belongs together without demanding identical text.
