If Sensation, Intuition, and Imagination Interact, Can One Architecture Keep Them From Collapsing Into Each Other?

FractiAI Research — October 23, 2026

A hungry child does not experience “interoception,” “prediction error,” and “caregiver learning” as separate academic shelves. A partner who feels a sudden gut caution before a difficult conversation does not automatically know whether that impression is hard-won pattern recognition, stress, or bias. A designer who can hear an unplayed melody in their head is not thereby claiming a new sense organ. Sex Expeditions 002 asks whether those kinds of experiences—physical sensation, bodily signals, rapid judgment, and mental simulation—can sit in one testable map without being mashed into a single slogan about “the body knowing.”

The problem of collapsed variables

Everyday life rewards shortcuts. Clinics, classrooms, and relationship advice columns often treat desire, arousal, attraction, identity, fantasy, and behavior as if they were one dial. Workplaces confuse confidence with competence. Parents confuse a child’s vivid imagination with a report about the outside world. When variables collapse, bad decisions follow: someone mistakes a physiological response for consent, or treats an intuitive dislike as a finished fact about another person’s character. A job interview can turn the same confusion into a paycheck decision—mistaking a confident gut call for calibrated skill. Expedition 002’s first job is linguistic and measurement hygiene—keep the parts separate long enough to test how they connect.

What the architecture proposes

The paper proposes seven functional layers: biological substrate; signal detection and bodily state; integration and significance; sensory, cognitive, intuitive, and imaginative experience; interpretation and regulation; response and action; and learning and longer-term tuning. Alongside the layers sit five tuning dimensions—biological foundations, experience, their interaction, current context, and unresolved attribution—and a wide catalog from molecular portals to sexual and reproductive constructs, wellbeing, and social dynamics.

Importantly, the framework treats intuition as potentially informative but fallible, and imagination as the generation of mental representations—not as proof of extrasensory channels. Cognitive sensing is an umbrella for pattern noticing through ordinary cognitive processes, not a newly invented sense organ.

Hypothesis under test in the pilot

Before any human dataset, the expedition asks a narrow computational question: can a recursive multiscale state-transition idea be compared with simpler baselines on tracking error, smoothness, and recovery after a disturbance? The hypothesis is not that recursion must win. The hypothesis is that the comparison can be run with explicit metrics—and that ablations will reveal trade-offs rather than magic. That habit of pre-committing the scoreboard matters as much for rent-paying research budgets as it does for theory: mixed results are allowed to stay mixed.

What the 500-step pilot found

Under seed 20261023, five conditions shared the same synthetic target series and observation noise: a feedforward chain, an additive-memory model, a feedback controller, a recursive multiscale model, and a recursive ablation that removed the slow cross-scale term. Additive memory posted the lowest one-step tracking RMSE at about 0.1435. Recursive multiscale was smoother, with mean absolute step change about 0.0603 versus about 0.0862 for additive memory, but it was not the best tracker. Removing the slow term improved tracking RMSE relative to the full recursive model (about 0.1767 versus 0.3615) while the full recursive model remained smoother—exactly the kind of mixed ledger that should stop anyone from crowning complexity.

Those numbers are method results on an artificial series. They are not estimates of hunger, attraction, workplace judgment, or clinical regulation.

What the literature already owns

A targeted reading of systems biology, predictive processing, active inference, homeostasis and allostasis, control theory, multilevel psychology, and cognitive work on imagery and intuition shows that multiscale feedback and many named cognitive categories already exist. Expedition 002 therefore cannot claim novelty merely for saying “layers interact.” Its candidate contribution is a shared operational catalog, explicit construct separations (especially in sexual and reproductive measurement), and a comparative Goldilocks-style multi-objective region—still unproven against established baselines.

Framework value without a victory lap

If later systematic review and independent validation support the catalog, researchers and practitioners gain a common checklist: measure sensation separately from intuition, imagination separately from observation, confidence separately from accuracy, desire separately from behavior, and consent as its own ethical and empirical requirement. Rent still comes due. Relationships still require negotiation. The map’s value is whether it reduces category errors that cost people sleep, dignity, and trust.
