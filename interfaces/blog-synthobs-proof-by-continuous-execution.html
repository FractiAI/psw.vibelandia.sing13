Proof by Continuous Execution

SS Vibelandia — 2026-07-28 — plain speak · Fair Exchange

Peer review still matters. It also has a bandwidth problem. When the thing under review is a living software corpus — open repositories, deterministic test suites, a live deployment — a static PDF can only report what the authors say happened. Proof by Continuous Execution asks a sharper question: what if the system itself keeps proving it still works?

The July 28, 2026 whitepaper formalizes that idea for SynthOBS and Lattice Chat. Traditional review compresses multi-dimensional architectures into prose. Reviewers get tired. Latency stretches into months. Trust leans on narrative. A self-demonstrating corpus — codebase, deployment, and executable suite — shortens the falsification loop to the time it takes an assertion to fail.

El Gran Sol’s Fractal Constant (about 1.618) appears here as an architectural “golden key” for scale-invariant phase labels in the stated model, not as a replacement for Planck’s constant or a guarantee that every product sentence is true. The empirical suite reports a 9/9 pass on model fixtures. Companion headline scores remain design targets, not wet-lab refutations of journals.

Stakes are practical. Multi-agent systems and high-dimensional field models outgrow what a handful of human readers can execute in their heads. PCE does not abolish ethics review, clinical trials, or laboratory measurement. It argues that for executable systems, continuous public re-run can outrank passive trust in a manuscript — under stated assumptions.

The deeper cultural claim is modest and serious at once: science that ships as running code owes the public a way to poke it. Static peer review remains a complementary rubric — NSPFRNP Snap PRA still audits structure — not scrap. Continuous execution is the other half of the handshake.

Honesty boundary: architectural maps, catalog labels, and simulator grammar stay in their tier tables.
