FractiSI Multi-Agent: Freemium Pricing & Packaging

Professional infrastructure with a genuinely useful Community edition — not a low-cost alternative to Cursor, Claude Code, or other agent platforms.

Commercial principle: charge for the capacity to coordinate productive agent fleets, not for access to the underlying idea or how many times someone runs a command. Value rises with workload size and complexity: more agents, one shared world, composed outcomes.

FractiSI is not another AI coding assistant. It is the coordination and shared-world infrastructure that makes denser, more productive agent fleets possible. Customers should pay for that — and for agent density per infrastructure budget.

Why these prices

The market is professional developer infrastructure and agentic engineering — not inexpensive plugins. Cursor Premium (~$30), Copilot Pro+ (~$39), Claude Pro (~$20), and team seats (~$20–30+/user) sell AI experiences or productivity. FractiSI sells coordination infrastructure independent of which models or IDEs you use. That is a separate subscription.

We do not price at $9 or $19 to attract customers. $39 Developer Pro sits beside established professional tools; $149 Team sells organizational capability, not a cheap multi-seat bundle.

Pricing metric: coordinated agent capacity

Human seats are not the primary unit. Value tracks how many agents work concurrently in one shared world.

MetricCommercial treatment
Human operatorsIncluded allowance by edition
Registered agentsGenerous allowance — not the main billing unit
Concurrent coordinated agentsPrimary capacity dimension

One hundred registered agents with twelve working at once should not be billed as one hundred. Concurrent coordination capacity is what customers scale — without a punitive per-agent tax on density.

Edition progression

  • Community — Discover and build · download, install, experiment, benchmark, real local workloads
  • Developer Pro — Operate · larger fleet, multiple projects, interference, reliable composition
  • Team — Govern · multi-person fleets, ownership, policies, integration and release control
  • Enterprise — Institutionalize · own infra, identity, governance, capacity, deployment, SLAs

Packaging rules

  1. Community is genuinely useful — no crippled Tiny Footprint, no withheld core, no credit card to try.
  2. Don’t charge for model tokens — customers bring Cursor, Claude Code, OpenAI, or other access.
  3. Don’t charge per agent registration — charge concurrent capacity and organizational capability.
  4. Don’t make deployment mandatory — local compose + Git stay useful without a cloud subscription.
  5. Enterprise must earn its contract — private deploy, governance, support, and capacity.

Launch-stage honesty

Workstation 0.2 already ships the Community path (including real-workload try with optional deploy). Adapters normalize Cursor / Claude Code / VS Code messages; they do not yet auto-drive live IDE sessions. Some Team/Enterprise controls are commercial architecture ahead of full productization.

  • Keep this four-tier (+ Cloud) structure.
  • Community is available now.
  • Sell Developer Pro when paid-only features are operational and tested.
  • Sell Team when multi-user admin and governance work.
  • Sell Enterprise through validated deployment contracts.
  • Don’t charge for roadmap promises.

Lab receipts stay qualified; reproduce benchmarks on your hardware.

Annual & expansion

Annual commitments at the same effective monthly rate initially (no discount theater). Annual customers get defined support, capacity planning, priority updates, an architecture review, and predictable renewal. Concurrent-agent capacity packs may follow as real workloads establish economics.
