## Abstract

Geometry is often studied as shape and space independent of context. Natural and living systems, however, manifest geometry under host environments—constraints, resources, interactions, and history—while geometry may reciprocally alter the host.

This expedition asks whether **host dependence** and **cross-host relationship recurrence** can be separated as measurable questions, and whether a disciplined extension of fractal analysis (“relational fractality”) can be tested with explicit mappings and null models.

**Net findings.** Established literature supports fractal scaling (with caveats), network geometry, environmental modulation of networks, and multiscale organization. **GHDR-1** (executed) shows the same shortest-path objective yielding route length **28.00** on unobstructed terrain and **≈34.63** with a barrier (**≈23.7%** longer vs straight-line baseline; **5** direction changes). This demonstrates host-dependent geometric expression in a defined model only.

**Guiding proposition:** *We are equations, a fractal with a rhyme.* The scientific task is to specify the rhyme, measure host shaping, and test generalizability with prespecified mappings and null models—not visual analogy alone.
