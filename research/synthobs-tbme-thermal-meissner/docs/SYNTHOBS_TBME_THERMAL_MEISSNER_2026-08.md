Above $T_c$, thermal kinetic energy ($k_B T$) dominates the lattice. Phonon scattering randomizes conduction-electron phase trajectories: ohmic resistance ($R>0$) and free flux threading.

Below $T_c$, phonon scattering decays; electrons form Cooper pairs and condense into a macroscopic quantum wave-function. Two defining phenomena appear: $R=0$ and complete flux expulsion (Meissner–Ochsenfeld):

$$
B_{\mathrm{int}} = B_{\mathrm{ext}} + 4\pi M = 0 \quad \implies \quad \chi = \frac{M}{B_{\mathrm{ext}}} = -\frac{1}{4\pi}
$$

(cgs-form reminder as in many textbook presentations; SI uses $\mathbf{B}=\mu_0(\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{M})$ with $\chi=-1$ for perfect diamagnetism — **SI forms are authoritative for lab work**.)

The Meissner effect is not “frozen-in” flux from perfect conductivity alone; it is an active diamagnetic state. Screening currents within the London penetration depth $\lambda_L$ produce an opposing field that expels flux from the bulk.
